# Strength & Muscle

Muscle is not just an aesthetic concern — it is one of the most powerful predictors of longevity and health span. Maintaining and building lean muscle mass is among the highest-ROI interventions available, regardless of age.

---

## Why Muscle Is a Longevity Organ

**Muscle mass is strongly and independently associated with reduced all-cause mortality.** [STRONG]

> [Source: Srikanthan & Karlamangla (2014) — muscle mass index and mortality](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24612965/)

Beyond aesthetics, muscle:

- **Glucose disposal:** Skeletal muscle is the primary site of insulin-mediated glucose uptake. More muscle = better blood sugar regulation = lower metabolic disease risk [STRONG]
- **Protein reservoir:** During illness or injury, muscle is catabolized for substrate. Greater muscle mass provides a buffer against wasting [MODERATE]
- **Physical reserve:** The capacity to generate force, recover from falls, and remain functional at advanced age depends directly on muscle mass and strength built earlier in life [STRONG]
- **Resting metabolic rate:** Muscle tissue is metabolically active; each kilogram burns ~13 kcal/day at rest, supporting leanness [MODERATE]

Peter Attia frames this as the **"Centenarian Decathlon"**: what physical capabilities does your 85-year-old self need, and what must your 40-year-old self build to get there with normal age-related decline factored in?

> **Sarcopenia** (age-related muscle loss) begins in the 30s at ~3–5% per decade, accelerating after 60. Resistance training is the only intervention that meaningfully reverses it. [STRONG]

## Core Principles

### 1. Progressive Overload Is Non-Negotiable [STRONG]

Muscle grows in response to mechanical tension that exceeds what it is accustomed to. Without progressive overload — gradually increasing weight, volume, or difficulty — training maintains but does not build.

**Progressive overload methods:**
- Add weight (most straightforward)
- Add reps at the same weight
- Add sets
- Decrease rest time
- Improve range of motion on the same weight
- Increase speed/power

### 2. Minimum Effective Volume [STRONG]

More volume is not always better. Diminishing returns set in quickly:

| Sets per muscle group per week | Outcome |
|-------------------------------|---------|
| 0–4 sets | Maintenance possible |
| 5–10 sets | Near-maximal gains for most people |
| 10–20 sets | Additional gains, more recovery demand |
| 20+ sets | High responder territory; increased injury risk |

**For most goals: 10–15 working sets per muscle group per week, split across 2 sessions.** [STRONG]

> [Source: Schoenfeld et al. (2017) — dose-response relationship of resistance training volume](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28834797/)

### 3. Rep Range Is Flexible [STRONG]

For hypertrophy (muscle growth), a wide range of rep schemes work — provided sets are taken close to failure:

- 5–8 reps: Effective (heavier loads, more neural stimulus)
- 8–12 reps: Classic hypertrophy range, good balance of fatigue/stimulus
- 15–30 reps: Effective with lighter loads if taken close to failure

> [Source: Schoenfeld et al. (2021) — load and hypertrophy equivalence](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33769953/)

**Training to failure:** Not required, but proximity to failure matters. Stopping 2–3 reps before failure (RIR = reps in reserve) is as effective as failure training and reduces cumulative fatigue. [STRONG]

### 4. Compound Movements First

Compound lifts (multi-joint) train more muscle per unit of time and drive greater hormonal response than isolation exercises. They should anchor your training:

| Pattern | Primary Movement | Examples |
|---------|-----------------|----------|
| Hip hinge | Deadlift pattern | Romanian deadlift, trap bar deadlift |
| Squat | Knee dominant | Back squat, goblet squat, split squat |
| Push (horizontal) | Chest, shoulders, triceps | Bench press, push-up |
| Push (vertical) | Overhead press pattern | Overhead press, landmine press |
| Pull (horizontal) | Rows | Barbell row, cable row, dumbbell row |
| Pull (vertical) | Lat dominant | Pull-up, lat pulldown |
| Carry | Whole-body stability | Farmer carry, suitcase carry |

Isolation work (curls, lateral raises, calf raises) is useful accessory work but should not replace compound lifting.

### 5. Frequency: 2x/Week Per Muscle Group [STRONG]

Training each muscle group twice per week produces superior hypertrophy compared to once per week, at equivalent total volume. [STRONG]

> [Source: Schoenfeld et al. (2016) — training frequency meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26863021/)

**Example 3-day split (full body):**
- Day 1: Squat pattern, vertical pull, horizontal push, accessory
- Day 2: Hip hinge, horizontal pull, vertical push, accessory
- Day 3: Unilateral legs, carries, weak-point accessory

**Example 4-day upper/lower split:**
- Day 1 (Upper): Horizontal push, vertical pull, accessory
- Day 2 (Lower): Squat, hip hinge, single-leg
- Day 3 (Upper): Vertical push, horizontal pull, accessory
- Day 4 (Lower): Romanian deadlift, single-leg, carries

## Protein: The Rate-Limiting Factor

Resistance training without adequate protein does not produce optimal muscle growth. [STRONG]

**Protein targets:**

| Goal | Daily Intake |
|------|-------------|
| Maintenance (sedentary) | 0.8 g/kg body weight |
| Active / recreational | 1.4–1.6 g/kg |
| Building muscle | 1.6–2.2 g/kg |
| Over 60 / during fat loss | 2.0–2.4 g/kg (higher due to anabolic resistance) |

> [Source: Morton et al. (2018) — protein meta-analysis, upper limits of benefit](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/)

**Protein distribution matters:** Spreading intake across 3–4 meals (30–50g each) maximizes muscle protein synthesis via repeated leucine thresholds, compared to front-loading all protein in one or two meals. [MODERATE]

**Leucine threshold:** ~2.5–3g of leucine per meal is needed to maximally stimulate muscle protein synthesis. Most animal proteins (chicken, beef, eggs, fish) easily reach this threshold at 30–40g of total protein. Plant proteins have lower leucine density and may require higher total intake. [MODERATE]

## Lean Muscle vs. Bulking

For longevity and body composition (rather than bodybuilding), the goal is **lean mass accrual** — building muscle without significant fat gain.

**Practical approach:**
- Eat at a modest surplus of 100–300 kcal/day above maintenance
- Prioritize protein (2.0 g/kg minimum)
- Track body weight weekly (average of 7 days) — aim for 0.2–0.5kg/month gain
- If gaining faster, reduce surplus

For fat loss while preserving muscle (body recomposition):
- Mild caloric deficit (300–500 kcal below maintenance)
- High protein (2.2–2.4 g/kg) — protects muscle during deficit [STRONG]
- Continue resistance training — critical for muscle preservation during fat loss [STRONG]

> [Source: Helms et al. (2014) — protein in caloric deficit](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24092765/)

## Strength for Longevity: What to Prioritize

If you're training with longevity as the primary goal (rather than aesthetics), prioritize:

1. **Grip strength** — one of the best longevity biomarkers, correlated with heart disease and mortality [STRONG]
   > [Source: Leong et al. (2015) — grip strength and cardiovascular outcomes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25982160/)

2. **Leg press / squat strength** — lower body strength is strongly predictive of functional independence in older age [STRONG]

3. **Carries (loaded) and single-leg work** — train the stability and balance required for real-world movement

4. **Hip hinge pattern** — deadlift variants train posterior chain, critical for posture, lower back health, and power production

5. **Overhead strength** — shoulder health and pressing capacity predict upper body resilience

**Practical test:** Can you get off the floor from a seated position without using your hands or knees for support? Difficulty with this is associated with substantially higher mortality risk. [MODERATE]

## Recovery Between Sessions

Muscle is built during recovery, not during training. Insufficient recovery produces fatigue accumulation rather than adaptation.

**General guidelines:**
- Allow 48–72 hours before training the same muscle group with high intensity again [MODERATE]
- 7–9 hours of sleep strongly amplifies muscle protein synthesis [STRONG]
- Caloric restriction during recovery blunts adaptation; do not diet aggressively in phases of active building [MODERATE]
- Creatine monohydrate (3–5g/day) is the most evidence-supported legal supplement for strength and lean mass [STRONG]

> [Source: Rawson & Volek (2003) — creatine and resistance training meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14636102/)
