# DOMS & Soreness Management

Delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS) is not an injury — it's an inflammation response to mechanical stress your muscles haven't adapted to yet. Understanding the mechanism is key to managing it well.

---

## What DOMS Actually Is

DOMS peaks 24–72 hours after exercise and is caused primarily by **eccentric muscle contractions** — the lengthening-under-load phase (lowering a weight, running downhill, the descent in a squat). [STRONG]

The microscopic muscle fiber disruption triggers:
1. Local inflammation and prostaglandin release → pain
2. Fluid accumulation in the tissue → swelling and stiffness
3. Sensitization of pain receptors (not actual injury to nerves) → hypersensitivity to touch and pressure

> [Source: Cheung et al. (2003) — DOMS review, British Journal of Sports Medicine](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14514537/)

**Key fact:** Soreness is NOT a reliable indicator of muscle damage severity, training quality, or how much adaptation will occur. You can have a highly productive training session with minimal DOMS, and vice versa.

---

## Why Recovery Feels Slower Over Time

If your recovery is feeling noticeably slower than it used to, several factors are likely compounding:

### Accumulated Fatigue
Training stress is cumulative. If you've been hitting muscles hard for multiple consecutive weeks without a structured deload, systemic fatigue builds even if individual sessions feel manageable. The body falls behind on repair.

**Signs of accumulated fatigue:**
- Soreness that doesn't fully resolve between sessions
- Stiffness that lingers, especially in the mornings
- Joints feel more achey than muscles
- Performance flatlines or drops slightly
- Sleep feels less restorative

### The "Too Much Rest" Trap
Counterintuitively, **complete rest often makes DOMS worse and recovery slower**, not better. [MODERATE]

When you stop moving entirely:
- Blood flow to damaged tissue drops
- Lymphatic drainage (which clears inflammation byproducts) stalls — the lymphatic system has no pump; it relies on muscle movement
- Connective tissue stiffens when not loaded
- Metabolic waste products (lactate, inflammatory cytokines) clear more slowly

The result: you feel stiffer and more sore after a full rest day than after a light movement day.

### Dehydration and Sleep Deficit
Even mild chronic dehydration slows cellular repair. [MODERATE] Each 1% reduction in body water measurably impairs physiological function. Poor sleep — even 30–60 minutes less per night — significantly blunts protein synthesis and growth hormone release, both required for tissue repair. [STRONG]

## What Actually Helps

### Ranked by Evidence Strength

| Intervention | Evidence | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Light movement / active recovery | STRONG | Blood flow, lymphatic clearance |
| Sleep (7–9 hrs, quality) | STRONG | Protein synthesis, GH release |
| Adequate protein intake | STRONG | Substrate for repair |
| Cold water immersion | MODERATE | Reduces acute inflammation |
| Heat / sauna | MODERATE | Vasodilation, blood flow |
| Foam rolling / soft tissue work | MODERATE | Reduces perceived stiffness, improves ROM |
| Omega-3s / anti-inflammatory diet | MODERATE | Reduces systemic inflammation |
| NSAIDs (ibuprofen) | MIXED | Reduces pain short-term; may blunt adaptation long-term |
| Massage | MODERATE | Blood flow, parasympathetic activation |

---

## What Doesn't Help (or May Hurt)

**Static stretching during acute DOMS:** Does not reduce soreness and does not speed recovery. [STRONG] Stretching a damaged, inflamed muscle doesn't address the mechanism.

**NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) used routinely:** Blunt the prostaglandin response that is part of the adaptation signal. Regular use post-workout may impair muscle hypertrophy over time. [MODERATE] Use sparingly for severe cases; don't use as a routine recovery tool.

**Complete inactivity:** As described above — prolongs, not shortens, soreness. [MODERATE]

---

## Practical Protocol for Severe DOMS

1. **Day 1 post-hard session:** 20–30 min Zone 1 movement (walk, easy bike, swim) — enough to raise heart rate to ~100–110 bpm
2. **Day 2 (peak DOMS):** Same light movement + foam roll the affected areas 5–10 min + prioritize sleep and hydration
3. **Day 3–4:** Begin loading again at 50–60% intensity — re-exposing the muscle to movement accelerates adaptation
4. **Throughout:** 1.6–2.2g protein per kg bodyweight, 3+ liters water, 7–9 hrs sleep

> The "repeated bout effect" means the same workout produces significantly less DOMS the second time. Your body adapts quickly to the specific stimulus. [STRONG]

---

## When Soreness Is Not DOMS

DOMS is bilateral, distributed across a muscle belly, appears 24–72 hours post-exercise, and resolves within 5–7 days.

See a clinician if:
- Pain is sharp, localized, or unilateral
- Swelling is visible and severe
- Pain onset is immediate during exercise (not delayed)
- Urine turns dark brown (rhabdomyolysis — rare but serious)
- Soreness doesn't improve meaningfully after 7 days
