# Recovery Nutrition

What you eat (and when) directly determines how fast damaged muscle tissue repairs and whether adaptation happens at all. Nutrition is not a soft add-on to recovery — it's the substrate.

---

## Protein: The Foundation

Muscle protein synthesis (MPS) — the cellular process of repairing and building new muscle fibers — requires amino acids, full stop. No protein, no repair.

### How Much [STRONG]

**1.6–2.2g of protein per kg of bodyweight per day** is the range supported by meta-analysis for maximizing MPS and hypertrophy in training individuals. Going above 2.2g/kg provides no measurable additional benefit.

> [Source: Morton et al. (2018) — protein intake and hypertrophy meta-analysis, BJSM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/)

| Bodyweight | Minimum target | Optimal range |
|---|---|---|
| 70 kg (154 lbs) | 112g/day | 112–154g/day |
| 80 kg (176 lbs) | 128g/day | 128–176g/day |
| 90 kg (198 lbs) | 144g/day | 144–198g/day |

**Protein is the single most impactful nutritional lever for recovery speed.**

### Timing [MODERATE]

The anabolic window is wider than once believed. MPS is elevated for 24+ hours post-training, not just the 30–60 minute window once thought critical. [MODERATE]

That said:
- **Post-training (within 1–2 hours):** 30–40g of high-quality protein initiates MPS. Useful but not mandatory if daily protein targets are met. [MODERATE]
- **Before bed:** 30–40g of slow-digesting protein (casein — found in Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, milk) sustains overnight MPS during the long fasting period of sleep. [MODERATE]
- **Distribution:** Spreading protein across 3–5 meals (rather than front or back-loading) maximizes the MPS stimulus per dose.

### Protein Quality [STRONG]

**Leucine** is the primary trigger for MPS — it acts as a signal that protein is available. Animal proteins (meat, fish, eggs, dairy) are typically higher in leucine and more bioavailable. For plant-based diets, combining sources (legumes + grains) or supplementing with essential amino acids closes the gap.

**Whey protein** (from dairy) is rapidly digested and particularly effective peri-workout due to high leucine content.

## Carbohydrates for Recovery [STRONG]

Carbohydrates replenish muscle glycogen — the stored fuel depleted during training. Glycogen is required for high-intensity work and for the next session's performance.

**Post-training carbohydrate:**
- 1–1.5g/kg bodyweight within 2 hours after training restores glycogen effectively [STRONG]
- Carbohydrate + protein together produce a greater MPS response than protein alone, partly via insulin signaling [MODERATE]

**Practical:** A meal containing rice, potatoes, bread, or oats alongside a protein source covers this efficiently. No need for special supplements.

Carbohydrate is less critical for purely strength training (which depletes glycogen less than endurance work) than for mixed-modality or high-volume sessions.

---

## Hydration [STRONG]

Muscle tissue is ~75% water. Even mild dehydration slows nutrient delivery, impairs protein synthesis, and increases perceived soreness.

**Targets:**
- **Daily:** 3–4 liters for active individuals (urine should be pale yellow)
- **Post-training replacement:** ~1.5L per kg of bodyweight lost during training (weigh before and after to estimate)
- **Electrolytes:** Sodium, potassium, and magnesium are lost in sweat. For sessions >60 minutes or sessions in heat, electrolyte replacement matters alongside water.

---

## Anti-Inflammatory Nutrition [MODERATE]

Chronic systemic inflammation slows recovery and increases soreness persistence. Diet significantly modulates baseline inflammation levels.

**Pro-recovery nutrients:**
| Nutrient | Sources | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Omega-3 fatty acids (EPA/DHA) | Fatty fish (salmon, sardines, mackerel), fish oil | Reduces inflammatory cytokines, reduces DOMS [MODERATE] |
| Polyphenols / antioxidants | Berries, tart cherry, turmeric, dark leafy greens | Reduce oxidative stress post-exercise [MODERATE] |
| Vitamin D | Sunlight, fatty fish, fortified foods | Deficiency associated with impaired muscle function and slower recovery [MODERATE] |
| Magnesium | Nuts, seeds, leafy greens, legumes | Muscle relaxation, sleep quality, protein synthesis co-factor [MODERATE] |
| Zinc | Meat, shellfish, legumes | Protein synthesis, immune function, tissue repair [STRONG] |

**Tart cherry juice** has the most consistent evidence among single foods for reducing DOMS: 30mL concentrate twice daily for 4–5 days around hard training reduces perceived soreness and inflammatory markers. [MODERATE]

> [Source: Howatson et al. (2010) — tart cherry and DOMS, BJSM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19883392/)

**Foods to limit during hard training blocks:**
- Alcohol: Directly impairs protein synthesis, blunts HGH, disrupts sleep [STRONG]
- Ultra-processed foods high in refined seed oils: Increase systemic inflammation [MODERATE]

## Creatine Monohydrate [STRONG]

Creatine is the most researched supplement in sports science. Relevant to recovery:

- Increases phosphocreatine stores, enabling faster ATP resynthesis between efforts
- Reduces muscle cell damage markers post-exercise
- Some evidence for reduced DOMS with regular creatine use [MODERATE]
- Supports muscle protein synthesis alongside resistance training [STRONG]

**Protocol:** 3–5g/day of creatine monohydrate. No loading phase necessary. Take daily — timing relative to training is not critical.

## Practical Recovery Day Nutrition

A simple framework for optimizing recovery without complexity:

**Priority 1 — Hit protein targets.** If you do nothing else, getting 1.6g+ protein per kg bodyweight per day will have the largest impact on recovery speed.

**Priority 2 — Hydrate consistently.** Drink water before you're thirsty. By the time thirst kicks in, you're mildly dehydrated.

**Priority 3 — Eat real food.** Whole foods naturally provide the anti-inflammatory nutrients, micronutrients, and fiber that support tissue repair and gut health (which affects systemic inflammation).

**Priority 4 — Limit alcohol.** Even one drink measurably impairs the overnight recovery window.
