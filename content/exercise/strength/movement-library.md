# Movements & Equipment

The [Year One Gym Program](content/exercise/strength/gym-program.md) names one movement per slot. This page gives the substitutions — for when a rack is taken, a machine is missing, or a movement doesn't suit your joints.

**On gym inventory:** equipment varies by location, including between branches of the same chain. Vasa locations generally carry the full commercial-gym kit described below — power racks, barbells, a dumbbell range, cable towers, plate-loaded and selectorized machines, and a Smith machine — but confirm what your specific location has on your first visit rather than planning around it. Every movement here has at least one alternative that works with less equipment.

---

## The Seven Patterns

Everything in the program is one of seven movement patterns. If you understand the pattern, you can always find a substitute on a crowded floor.

| Pattern | Free weight primary | Free weight alt | Machine substitute |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Squat** | Back squat | Goblet squat, front squat | Hack squat, leg press, Smith squat |
| **Hip hinge** | Trap-bar deadlift | Romanian deadlift, KB swing | Back extension, seated leg curl |
| **Horizontal push** | Flat DB bench press | Barbell bench, push-up | Chest press machine |
| **Vertical push** | Standing overhead press | Seated DB press, landmine press | Shoulder press machine |
| **Horizontal pull** | Chest-supported row | Single-arm DB row, barbell row | Seated cable row |
| **Vertical pull** | Pull-up | Chin-up | Lat pulldown, assisted pull-up machine |
| **Carry / anti-rotation** | Farmer carry | Suitcase carry | Cable Pallof press |

Missing a slot entirely is worse than substituting imperfectly. If the rack is occupied for twenty minutes, take the leg press and move on.

## Free Weights vs. Machines

Not a hierarchy — they do different jobs, and the program deliberately uses both.

**Free weights are better for:**
- Building the coordination and stabilization that transfers outside the gym
- Loading the whole system, which is where the neural adaptation of relative strength comes from
- Training in ranges of motion your joints actually choose

**Machines are better for:**
- Loading a muscle close to failure safely, with no spotter and no balance requirement
- Training around a cranky joint by fixing the path
- High-quality accessory volume when you're already fatigued from the main lift
- Coming back from a layoff, before pattern quality has returned

**Where they belong in this program:** free weights for the first one or two movements each session, machines for accessories. Block 1 inverts this — lean on machines early while your technique and tendons catch up, then migrate the main lifts to free weights during Block 2.

> [Source: Schoenfeld et al. (2021) — load and hypertrophy across modalities](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33769953/) [STRONG]

## Notes on Specific Movements

### Trap-bar deadlift
The default hinge for this program rather than the conventional barbell deadlift. The handles sit beside you rather than in front, which shortens the lever on your lower back and makes it substantially easier to load safely without a coach watching. If your gym has one, use it.

### Back squat vs. leg press
The back squat trains more, transfers better, and is worth learning properly. But it also demands ankle and thoracic mobility that a returning lifter may not have yet. If depth is poor, run goblet squats and leg press through Block 1 while working [Ankles](content/exercise/mobility/ankles.md) and [Thoracic Spine](content/exercise/mobility/thoracic-spine.md), and introduce the barbell in Block 2.

### Pull-ups
If you can't do 6 clean reps, use the assisted pull-up machine rather than the lat pulldown — assisted pull-ups keep the actual movement pattern while reducing load, which is what you want to progress. Reduce assistance over time rather than adding reps.

### Overhead press
The lift where 5 lb jumps are too large for most people. This is the strongest argument for microplates. Standing is preferable to seated — it trains the bracing that makes the lift useful — but seated is a fine substitute if lower back position degrades.

### Smith machine
Fine for accessories, poor for main lifts. The fixed bar path removes the stabilization demand that's a large part of why heavy compound lifting produces the strength you're after. Use it for split squats or incline press when the floor is crowded; don't build your squat on it.

### Carries
The most underrated item in the program and the one people skip. Grip strength is among the better-validated correlates of long-term health outcomes, and carries train it while also loading the trunk under real conditions.

> [Source: Leong et al. (2015) — grip strength and cardiovascular outcomes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25982160/) [STRONG]

If your gym has no open floor for walking carries, do a loaded static hold: heaviest dumbbells you can hold, 30–45 seconds.

## Full Range of Motion

Every rep goes through the complete range the movement allows — full depth on squats, full stretch on Romanian deadlifts, dead hang at the bottom of pull-ups, full lockout overhead.

Partial reps let you use more weight, which feels like progress and isn't. Full-range training is also what keeps a strength program from costing you flexibility — see [Strength & Flexibility](content/exercise/mobility/strength-and-flexibility.md) and [Lengthened Position Training](content/exercise/mobility/lengthened-position-training.md).

**If you can't reach full range on a movement, the load is wrong or the mobility isn't there yet.** Both are fixable. Neither is fixed by shortening the rep.

## A Minimum Session

For days when the gym is packed, you're short on time, or motivation is thin. Three movements, twenty-five minutes, and it counts as a session:

1. One squat or hinge — 3 × 5
2. One push — 3 × 6
3. One pull — 3 × 8

Showing up and doing this beats skipping, by a wide margin. Consistency across fifty-two weeks is what produces the outcome; no individual session matters much.
