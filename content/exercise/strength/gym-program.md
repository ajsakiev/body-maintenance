# Year One Gym Program

A twelve-month resistance training plan built for **relative strength** — getting substantially stronger while staying lean, at 2–3 sessions per week, using the free weights and machines available on any commercial gym floor.

Written for a returning lifter: someone who has trained before but not recently. The mechanism behind the design is in [Relative Strength](content/exercise/strength/relative-strength.md); exercise selection and machine substitutions are in [Movements & Equipment](content/exercise/strength/movement-library.md).

---

## The Design in One Table

| Variable | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 2–3 sessions/week | Every session is full body, so 2 days still hits each pattern twice |
| Session length | 45–60 min | Long rests, few exercises — quality over accumulation |
| Main lift reps | 3–6 | Heavy enough to drive neural adaptation |
| Accessory reps | 8–12 | Joint-friendly volume, some tissue building |
| Sets per muscle/week | 8–12 | Enough to build slowly, not enough to demand a surplus |
| Rest on main lifts | 2–3 min | Non-negotiable — short rest converts this into a different program |
| Proximity to failure | 1–3 reps in reserve | Rarely to failure; fatigue blunts force output |
| Calories | Maintenance | The actual lever controlling size |

## Weekly Structure

Every session is full body. On a 2-day week you run **A** and **B**. On a 3-day week you add **C**.

Order matters: heavy compound first while fresh, then secondary compounds, then accessories, then carries or core.

### Day A — Squat Emphasis

| # | Movement | Sets × Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Back squat *(or goblet / hack squat)* | 4 × 5 | 3 min |
| 2 | Flat dumbbell bench press | 3 × 6–8 | 2 min |
| 3 | Chest-supported row *(or seated cable row)* | 3 × 8–10 | 90 sec |
| 4 | Romanian deadlift | 3 × 8 | 90 sec |
| 5 | Farmer carry | 3 × 40 yd | 60 sec |

### Day B — Hinge Emphasis

| # | Movement | Sets × Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trap-bar deadlift *(or conventional deadlift)* | 4 × 5 | 3 min |
| 2 | Overhead press, barbell or dumbbell | 3 × 5–6 | 2 min |
| 3 | Pull-up, assisted pull-up, or lat pulldown | 3 × 6–8 | 2 min |
| 4 | Bulgarian split squat *(or walking lunge)* | 3 × 8 per leg | 90 sec |
| 5 | Cable face pull | 3 × 12–15 | 60 sec |
| 6 | Suitcase carry *(or Pallof press)* | 3 × 30 yd | 60 sec |

### Day C — Balance & Accessory *(3-day weeks only)*

| # | Movement | Sets × Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Front squat *(or leg press)* | 3 × 6–8 | 2 min |
| 2 | Incline dumbbell press *(or weighted dip)* | 3 × 6–8 | 2 min |
| 3 | Single-arm dumbbell row | 3 × 8–10 per arm | 90 sec |
| 4 | Hip thrust *(or back extension)* | 3 × 10 | 90 sec |
| 5 | Lateral raise / hammer curl / triceps pushdown | 2 × 12 each | 45 sec |
| 6 | Side plank + dead bug | 2 rounds | — |

**Scheduling:** never two sessions back to back if you can avoid it. Mon/Thu for two days; Mon/Wed/Fri or Tue/Thu/Sat for three. If life compresses the week, prioritize keeping the gap over hitting the exact days.

**Weekly volume this produces:** roughly 9–12 sets for back, 6–9 for chest and shoulders, 7–10 for quads and posterior chain — squarely in the relative-strength band.

## The Twelve-Month Arc

Six blocks. Each block changes the loading scheme; the exercises stay largely the same, because the strength comes from progressing on movements you've grooved rather than from novelty.

| Block | Weeks | Focus | Main lift | Accessories | RIR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Re-entry** | 1–6 | Technique, tendon tolerance | 3 × 8 | 2–3 × 10–12 | 3–4 |
| **2. Base** | 7–18 | Linear progression | 4 × 6 | 3 × 8–10 | 2–3 |
| **3. Strength** | 19–30 | Heavy neural work | 5 × 3–5 | 3 × 8–10 | 1–2 |
| **4. Density** | 31–42 | Undulating heavy/moderate | 4 × 4 and 3 × 8 alternating | 3 × 10–12 | 1–2 |
| **5. Peak & test** | 43–48 | Wave to a 3–5 rep max | 3–5 × 2–3 | 2 × 10 | 0–1 |
| **6. Reset** | 49–52 | Deload, then restart at a higher baseline | 3 × 8 light | 2 × 12 | 4 |

### Block 1 — Re-entry (Weeks 1–6)

The highest-risk block, for a reason that isn't obvious: your nervous system remembers loads your tendons and connective tissue no longer tolerate. Strength returns in weeks; tendon adaptation takes months. Most returning-lifter injuries happen here, chasing an old number.

- Start at roughly **50–60% of what you think you can lift.** You will feel underworked. That is correct.
- 3 × 8 on main lifts, stopping 3–4 reps short of failure
- Lean on machines more heavily this block — they let you load safely while pattern quality returns
- Add weight only when the previous session felt genuinely easy
- Expect soreness in weeks 1–2, then a rapid drop-off. See [DOMS & Soreness](content/exercise/recovery/doms-soreness.md)

### Block 2 — Base (Weeks 7–18)

Twelve weeks of steady linear progression. This is where most of your year's strength is actually built.

- 4 × 6 on main lifts, 2–3 RIR
- **Add weight every session or every other session** while it keeps moving: 5 lb on upper-body lifts, 10 lb on lower-body lifts
- Shift the primary lifts to free weights now if you started on machines
- When a weight stalls twice, stop adding and switch to double progression (below)

### Block 3 — Strength (Weeks 19–30)

The block that most directly produces the small-but-strong outcome.

- 5 × 3–5 on the main lift, 1–2 RIR, **full 3-minute rests**
- Accessories stay at 3 × 8–10 — this is what keeps you from becoming strong but fragile
- Move with intent: the bar should move fast even when it's heavy. Speed of intent is the training variable here
- Progression is now double progression, not weekly loading

### Block 4 — Density (Weeks 31–42)

Alternate heavy and moderate exposures to keep progressing without accumulating the fatigue that a straight heavy block eventually produces.

- Day A heavy (4 × 4), Day B moderate (3 × 8), alternating each week
- Accessories drift up to 3 × 10–12 — a slight volume bump for a bit of tissue
- Add a second carry or loaded-stability piece per session

### Block 5 — Peak & Test (Weeks 43–48)

- Waves of 3–5 × 2–3 at high load, 0–1 RIR on the last set only
- In week 48, **test a 5-rep max — not a 1-rep max.** A 5RM gives you nearly the same information about progress with a fraction of the injury risk and no need for spotters you don't have
- Log the numbers. These become year two's starting point

### Block 6 — Reset (Weeks 49–52)

- Two weeks genuinely light — half the sets, 50% of the weight, full range, no strain
- Two weeks rebuilding at Block 1 intensities, but with Block 2 weights
- Then restart the cycle. Year two runs the same arc from a higher floor

## Progression Rules

Three rules govern the whole year. Everything else is detail.

**1. Double progression.** For any exercise with a rep range (say 3 × 8–10): work up until you hit the *top* of the range on *every* set. Next session, add the smallest available increment and drop back to the bottom of the range. Climb again.

```
Session 1:  135 × 8, 8, 8
Session 2:  135 × 9, 9, 8
Session 3:  135 × 10, 10, 10   → add weight
Session 4:  145 × 8, 8, 7
```

**2. Increments.** 5 lb on upper-body lifts, 10 lb on lower-body. If those feel too large — common on overhead press — buy a pair of 1.25 lb microplates. They're about $20 and will add more to your year than any supplement.

**3. Deload every sixth week.** Keep the weight, halve the sets. Non-negotiable, and most useful precisely in the weeks you feel strongest — the point is to arrive at the next block fresh, not to recover from damage already done.

**When to deviate:** if you miss a rep target two sessions running on the same lift, don't push a third time. Drop the weight 10% and climb back. Stalling twice is information, not failure.

## Reps In Reserve

The whole program is autoregulated by RIR — how many more reps you could have done. Learn to read it, because it's what keeps the program adjusting to your sleep, stress, and recovery without you having to recalculate anything.

| RIR | Feels like | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| 4+ | Comfortable, could do many more | Warm-ups, Block 1, deloads |
| 3 | Clearly submaximal | Re-entry, accessories early in a block |
| 2 | Bar slows slightly on the last rep | The default working set |
| 1 | Last rep is a genuine grind | Top sets in Blocks 3–5 |
| 0 | Could not have done another | Rare — test weeks only |

If you consistently need 0 RIR to hit your numbers, the weight is too heavy for the block you're in.

## Session Structure

**Warm-up — 8 minutes, every session:**
1. 5 min easy bike or rower — raise core temperature, nothing more
2. 3–4 movements from the [Daily Mobility Routine](content/exercise/mobility/daily-routine.md), biased to the day's pattern
3. Ramp-up sets on the first lift: empty bar × 10, then ~40% × 5, ~60% × 3, ~80% × 1 before your working weight

The ramp-up sets are not optional and they don't count as working sets. They are how you find out what today's body is capable of before you commit to a number.

**Between sets:** actually rest. Set a timer. Two to three minutes feels absurdly long at first and is the difference between this program working and not.

**After:** nothing required. If you want to add [Zone 2](content/exercise/cardio/zone2-training.md) work, put it on non-lifting days, or after lifting — never before.

## What to Track

Four numbers per exercise, in any notes app. A spreadsheet is nice; a napkin works.

```
Date | Exercise | Weight | Reps per set | RIR on last set
```

That's it. You need enough to answer one question at the start of each session: *what did I do last time, and can I beat it?* Reviewing the log every six weeks — at each deload — is where you'll notice stalls that are invisible session to session.

## The Nutrition Guardrails

Training determines whether you get stronger. **Diet determines whether you get bigger.** Getting this wrong is the only way to end up with an outcome you didn't want.

- **Calories at maintenance** — weigh yourself weekly, use the 7-day average, and hold it within about 2 lb. If the average climbs steadily, eat less
- **Protein 1.6–2.0 g/kg bodyweight** — the floor for building and retaining muscle. See [Strength & Muscle](content/exercise/strength-muscle.md) for the evidence
- **If you want more definition,** run a mild deficit (300 kcal) during Block 2 or Block 4, not Block 3 or 5. Strength blocks need fuel
- **Creatine monohydrate, 5 g/day** — the one supplement with strong evidence behind it. It adds some intracellular water weight, which is not fat and not size in the sense you're avoiding

> [Source: Morton et al. (2018) — protein intake and resistance training outcomes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/) [STRONG]

## Missed Time

You will miss weeks. The plan assumes it.

| Time off | What to do |
|---|---|
| Up to 1 week | Nothing. Resume where you left off |
| 2–3 weeks | Drop 10%, rebuild over two sessions |
| 4–8 weeks | Drop 20%, repeat the last two weeks of your current block |
| 2+ months | Return to Block 1 at Block 2 weights. It'll take ~3 weeks to be back |

Detraining is much slower than people fear — strength persists far longer than the fitness industry implies. Do not restart from zero, and do not try to pick up exactly where you stopped.

## What Year One Should Actually Look Like

Realistic outcomes for a returning lifter at 2–3 days per week, eating at maintenance:

- **Main lifts up 40–70%** from your week-1 working weights — most of it in Blocks 2 and 3
- **Bodyweight roughly stable**, ±5 lb
- **Visible change concentrated in shoulders, upper back, and arms** — the areas where small amounts of muscle change your outline most
- **Waist unchanged or slightly smaller** if you keep calories honest
- Sizeable improvements in posture and how heavy ordinary things feel

What it will *not* look like: dramatic month-to-month visual change. The first four months are mostly neural, which means the numbers move well before the mirror does. This is the phase where people quit. Track the log, not the mirror.

## When to Back Off

- **Sharp joint pain** during a lift — stop that lift for the session and substitute a machine version. Sharp is different from sore
- **Strength dropping across consecutive sessions** while sleep and food are normal — you need a deload, not more effort
- **Resting heart rate up 7+ bpm for several days**, or sleep degrading — systemic fatigue; take the deload early
- **Any lower back pain that refers down a leg** — stop hinging and deadlifting, see someone about it

See [Recovery & Sleep](content/exercise/recovery-sleep.md) and [Active vs. Passive Recovery](content/exercise/recovery/active-vs-passive.md).
