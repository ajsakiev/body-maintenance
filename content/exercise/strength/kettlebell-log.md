## How to Log a Benchmark

After the test, report these four things:

- **Bell** — the weight, e.g. 16 kg
- **Score** — full rounds plus how far into the next round you got, e.g. "11 rounds + 10 swings + 3 squats"
- **Nasal?** — whether you breathed through your nose the whole 20 minutes
- **Notes** — anything that affected the day: bad sleep, a sore back, a different time of day

---

## How the Columns Work

**Score** is what you report. **Total reps** turns that score into a single number so partial rounds count fairly. One full round of Session 1 is **30 reps**: 10 swings + 5 squats + 5 push-ups + 10 rows (5 per side).

> Example: 11 rounds + 10 swings + 3 squats = (11 × 30) + 10 + 3 = **343 reps**

**vs. Week 1** is the percent change in total reps from your first test. The program's expectation is **+20–35%** by week 12, with most of it showing up by week 8.

**Nasal the whole way?** matters because the Benchmark is an effort-capped test. A higher score from mouth breathing means you tried harder, not that you got fitter, so it isn't a fair comparison. Mark it "No" and treat that score with caution.

---

## When the Bell Changes

At **15 rounds** the Benchmark moves up one bell size. Your score will drop by about a quarter. That's expected, not a step backward. A score at a new bell can't be compared to scores at the old one, so the bell move starts a fresh baseline: a new "vs." comparison, and a new cycle table when you restart the arc.
