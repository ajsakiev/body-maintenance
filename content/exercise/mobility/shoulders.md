# Shoulder Mobility

The shoulder is the most mobile joint in the body — and therefore the most vulnerable. Most people have significant restriction in shoulder flexion (overhead reach) and external rotation, which forces impingement of the rotator cuff tendons and contributes to neck and upper back pain.

> **Pilates note:** Pilates emphasizes scapular stability and shoulder retraction, which is excellent. What it rarely addresses is the glenohumeral joint itself — the ball-and-socket — and its full range of motion in overhead and behind-the-back planes. These exercises fill that gap.

---

## Self-Assessment

**Overhead Reach Test**
Stand with your back flat against a wall (whole spine touching). Raise both arms overhead trying to touch the wall.
- Both arms reach the wall without arching lower back: good overhead mobility
- Cannot reach without arching or arms stop 6–8 inches from wall: restriction in shoulder flexion or thoracic extension

**Internal Rotation Test (Apley Scratch)**
Reach one hand up behind your back (like scratching your upper back). How high does your thumb reach?
- Normal: thumb reaches mid-thoracic spine (T5–T8 region)
- Restricted: thumb only reaches lower back

**External Rotation Test**
With elbow at 90°, upper arm at your side, rotate the forearm outward. How far does it go?
- Normal: 80–90°
- Restricted: < 60° — common, especially in desk workers

## Exercises

### 1. Shoulder CARs (Controlled Articular Rotations)

**What it targets:** Full glenohumeral joint range; joint health maintenance  
**Time:** 1 min/side  
**When:** Every morning — the most important shoulder exercise to do daily  
**Video:** [Controlled Articular Rotations (CARs) — Shoulder](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CLWFwun1BfQ)

**How to do it:**
1. Stand with feet shoulder-width; brace your core and squeeze your glutes to lock the trunk still
2. Lift one arm to the side and begin tracing the largest possible circle with the hand
3. Go: forward → up → back behind the ear → down behind the back → back to start
4. **Keep the movement at the shoulder only** — no trunk rotation or compensation
5. The thumb faces up on the way forward; rotates so the thumb faces back on the overhead/behind-the-body portion
6. 3–5 reps each direction

**What to feel:** The shoulder working hard to reach positions it rarely visits. You should feel the joint "working" — not pain, but effort.

> Shoulder CARs maintain glenohumeral joint health and synovial fluid distribution. Daily practice is among the best investments for long-term shoulder longevity. [MODERATE]

---

### 2. Banded Shoulder Distraction

**What it targets:** Glenohumeral joint capsule; the restriction that limits overhead range  
**Time:** 90 sec/side  
**When:** Before shoulder work; when shoulders feel compressed or tight  
**Video:** [Banded Shoulder Distraction](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dO4Bc0xr9kU)

**Setup:** Resistance band anchored at shoulder height or above (door anchor or pull-up bar).

**How to do it:**
1. Face the anchor point
2. Put your arm through the loop so the band sits at the top of the upper arm (just below the shoulder)
3. Step back until there's moderate tension pulling your shoulder toward the anchor
4. Keep the arm straight and explore: reach overhead, reach across body, rotate — the band provides distraction of the joint while you move
5. Hold end-range positions for 5–10 sec; explore different angles

**Why it works:** The band distracts (pulls apart) the humeral head from the glenoid, creating space for the joint to move through ranges that capsular tightness normally blocks. [MODERATE]

---

### 3. Wall Slide (Serratus + Overhead Mobility)

**What it targets:** Serratus anterior activation + overhead shoulder range  
**Time:** 2 min  
**When:** Before Pilates or any upper body work; daily is fine  
**Video:** [Wall Slides for Serratus Anterior Activation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oMSVe7PWJ3o)

**How to do it:**
1. Stand facing a wall, forearms on the wall at shoulder height (elbows 90°)
2. Press forearms *into* the wall — this activates serratus anterior (the "saw-tooth" muscle that protracts the scapula)
3. Maintaining that pressing pressure, slide your arms upward along the wall as high as possible
4. Hold the top position for 3–5 sec; slide back down
5. 8–10 reps

**What to feel:** The area underneath and around the shoulder blade working. Not shrugging the traps.

> Weakness or inhibition of the serratus anterior is one of the most common contributors to shoulder impingement and "winging" of the scapula. [STRONG]

---

### 4. Doorway Pec Stretch with PAILs

**What it targets:** Pectoralis minor and anterior capsule — restricts shoulder external rotation and overhead reach  
**Time:** 2–3 min/side  
**When:** After periods of desk work; post-Pilates; evening  
**Video:** [Best Way To Stretch Your Pecs — Pec Stretch With PAILs & RAILs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m3yErtawRAs)

**How to do it:**
1. Stand in a doorway, forearm against the doorframe at 90° (upper arm parallel to floor)
2. Step through the doorway until you feel a stretch in the front of the chest/shoulder
3. Hold 30–45 sec (passive phase)

**PAILs add-on:**
4. Push your forearm forward into the doorframe as hard as you can without moving. 10 seconds at 50–70% effort. Breathe out.
5. Release and try to step further through the doorway, deepening the stretch.
6. Hold the new end-range for 10 seconds.
7. 2–3 sets/side.

**Arm variations:**
- Elbow at 90° (shoulder level) → stretches pec minor and anterior capsule
- Arm higher (forearm above head) → stretches more of the pec major upper fibers

---

### 5. Sleeper Stretch (Internal Rotation)

**What it targets:** Posterior shoulder capsule; restricts internal rotation  
**Time:** 60–90 sec/side  
**When:** Post-workout; evening; if you notice asymmetrical internal rotation  
**Video:** [Shoulder Posterior Capsule Stretch — Side Lying Sleeper Stretch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TVLLzEsKamA)

**How to do it:**
1. Lie on your side, shoulder directly below you on the floor
2. Extend the arm out to the side at shoulder height, elbow at 90°
3. Use your other hand to gently push the forearm *toward the floor* (rotating the shoulder internally)
4. There should be a stretch deep in the back of the shoulder — not pain
5. Hold 60–90 sec

**Important:** If you feel pinching or pain at the front of the shoulder, stop. This stretch is only for posterior capsule tightness. If you have existing shoulder issues, consult a physio before doing this.

---

### 6. Thread the Needle

**What it targets:** Thoracic rotation + shoulder mobility through the back  
**Time:** 2 min  
**When:** Morning; pre-Pilates; any time the upper back feels locked  
**Video:** [Thread the Needle Exercise for the Thoracic Spine](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uenqxybA9kU)

**How to do it:**
1. All fours position (hands under shoulders, knees under hips)
2. Take one hand off the floor and "thread" it through the gap between your body and the other arm, reaching across the floor
3. Let your shoulder drop toward the floor; rotate your thoracic spine
4. Pause at end range for 5 sec
5. Return and reach the same arm up toward the ceiling, opening the chest
6. 8–10 reps each side

> This doubles as a thoracic mobility exercise. The shoulder and thoracic spine are functionally linked — thoracic stiffness directly limits overhead shoulder range. See Thoracic Spine page for dedicated thoracic work.

## Weekly Prescription

| Priority | Exercise | Duration | When |
|---------|----------|----------|------|
| Daily | Shoulder CARs | 2 min | Morning |
| 4×/week | Wall slide | 3 min | Before upper body work |
| 3×/week | Doorway pec stretch + PAILs | 4 min | After desk work |
| 3×/week | Banded distraction | 3 min | Pre-session |
| 2×/week | Sleeper stretch | 3 min | Post-workout |
| 2×/week | Thread the needle | 3 min | Morning or mid-day |

> Most shoulder mobility restrictions are anterior capsule + pec tightness combined with serratus weakness. Daily CARs + consistent doorway stretch work is the highest-ROI combination. [MODERATE]
