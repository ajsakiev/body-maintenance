# Lengthened Position Training

Training a muscle in its **lengthened position** means loading it when it is at or near its maximum stretch — the end of its range of motion, not the middle. This is where most training programs are weakest, and where the most flexibility and injury-resilience gains are available.

---

## Why This Matters

Most conventional exercises train muscles primarily in their **shortened or mid-range position**. A standard leg press stops short of full hip and knee flexion. A lat pulldown starts from a full stretch but people rarely use that range intentionally. Bicep curls peak at 90 degrees, not the fully extended arm.

When a muscle is never loaded at full length:
- The nervous system treats that range as uncontrolled and unsafe — it restricts access to it
- The muscle's functional length shortens over time
- Tendons and connective tissue adapt to being loaded only in a shortened range, making the lengthened position feel stiff and vulnerable
- Flexibility you gain from passive stretching doesn't transfer to active control

**Lengthened-position training solves all of this at once** — it builds strength where you need it for mobility, and teaches the nervous system that full range is safe. [MODERATE]

> [Source: Kassiano et al. (2022) — training at longer muscle lengths and hypertrophy, Journal of Strength & Conditioning Research](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36018847/)

---

## How to Read This Guide

Each section covers:
- **What the lengthened position is** for that muscle
- **Key exercises** that load it there
- **Why it matters** specifically for that area

Integrate these as *part of* your strength training, not as extra mobility work added on top. The goal is making your training itself develop functional range.

---

## Posterior Chain

### Hamstrings

**Lengthened position:** Hip flexed (leg forward / torso forward), knee extended — like the bottom of a toe touch.

The hamstring crosses two joints (hip and knee). Most training only addresses the knee function. The hip function — pulling the torso upright from a hinged position — is where length is most limited in most people.

#### Romanian Deadlift (RDL)
The gold standard for hamstring length under load. The hip hinge position with a neutral spine stretches the hamstrings from the hip while maintaining tension throughout.

**Key technique:**
- Hip hinge, not squat — push hips back, slight knee bend, torso descends
- Lower the bar along your legs until you feel a strong hamstring stretch (typically just below the knee for most people; deeper with more flexibility)
- **Do not round the lower back** to reach deeper — the stretch should be from the hip, not spinal flexion
- Pause at the bottom for 1–2 seconds, then drive hips forward to return

**Load the stretch:** Slow eccentrics (3–4 seconds lowering) dramatically increase the training stimulus at the lengthened position.

#### Nordic Hamstring Curl
One of the most researched exercises for hamstring strength and injury prevention. Loads the hamstring **eccentrically at full length** as the knee extends under bodyweight load.

**Why it's important:** Most hamstring injuries occur during the eccentric phase at or near full extension (sprinting, kicking). The Nordic curl directly trains this position. [STRONG]

**Technique:**
- Kneel with feet anchored (under a bar, sofa, or partner)
- Lower your body toward the floor by allowing the knees to extend — resist with your hamstrings
- Lower as slowly as possible; use hands to catch yourself at the bottom
- Progress: add assistance (band) on the way down, then work toward unassisted

#### Good Morning
Similar hip hinge mechanics to the RDL but with a barbell on the back, which shifts emphasis to spinal erectors and glutes while still training the hamstrings through hip flexion range.

---

### Glutes

**Lengthened position:** Hip fully flexed (thigh toward chest) — opposite to the contracted position (hip extension, e.g., top of a hip thrust).

Most glute training (hip thrusts, cable kickbacks) loads the **shortened position** of the glute maximally. The deep squat, by contrast, loads the glute at its most stretched position.

#### ATG Squat (Ass-to-Grass)
The deep squat with full hip flexion is one of the few exercises that trains the glute at maximum stretch. Going to parallel or stopping short misses this.

**Key technique:**
- Full depth: hip crease below knee level at minimum; ideally thighs past parallel
- Knees track over toes — not caving inward
- Heel elevation (small plate or squat wedge) allows deeper depth while building ankle mobility in parallel
- The bottom position should feel like a controlled stretch, not a collapse

#### Deep Step-Up
Step up onto a box high enough that the working hip is at or past 90 degrees of flexion at the start position. This loads the glute in its lengthened position as you drive up.

---

### Spinal Erectors (Lower Back)

**Lengthened position:** Full spinal flexion — a rounded-forward position. Most people never load this position.

#### Jefferson Curl
The most direct way to build active strength in spinal flexion. Often feared, but when loaded progressively, it builds the posterior chain and spinal musculature at full length. [MODERATE]

**Technique:**
- Stand on a box or elevated surface, holding a light weight (start with 5–10kg or less)
- Slowly curl the spine forward one vertebra at a time — chin to chest, upper back rounds, lower back follows
- Lower the weight toward the floor in a fully rounded spine position
- Hold the stretched position 1–2 seconds; uncurl slowly
- **Progress load very slowly** — this is a new range for most people and the tissue needs time to adapt

**Important:** This is an *advanced* exercise. Work without load first, then progress conservatively. It is appropriate for healthy spines; avoid with herniated discs or active lower back pathology.

## Quadriceps

**Lengthened position:** Hip extended (leg behind body) AND knee flexed simultaneously — like a deep lunge or the position at the top of a leg curl.

The rectus femoris (one of the four quad muscles) crosses both hip and knee, so it is only fully stretched when both are in their lengthened position simultaneously. This is different from the other three quad heads.

#### Reverse Nordic Curl
The most direct exercise for quadriceps in their lengthened position. Essentially the opposite of the Nordic curl — the knee extends while the hip stays extended.

**Technique:**
- Kneel upright with feet anchored
- Lean back from the knee (not the hip) — keeping hips extended and back straight
- Lower your torso toward the floor while maintaining the lean
- This is extremely difficult; most people can only go 10–30 degrees back initially
- Use hands on the floor or a band for assistance

**Why it matters:** Strengthens the quad in the stretched position, addresses anterior knee pain, and builds the active hip extension + knee flexion range of motion needed for sprinting and jumping mechanics.

#### ATG Split Squat (Knee Over Toe Split Squat)
The rear leg hip flexor is in a stretched position; the front leg quad is loaded through a deep range including significant knee flexion.

**Technique:**
- Long stance; rear knee approaches the floor
- Drive the front knee forward over (and past) the toes
- Heel stays down on front foot
- Upright torso

#### Sissy Squat
Trains the quad eccentrically through a large range of knee flexion with hip extension. Intense — progress load carefully.

## Hip Flexors

**Lengthened position:** Hip fully extended (thigh behind body), often combined with slight hip external rotation. Most people never load this.

Hip flexors are perpetually shortened by sitting — making this one of the most important areas to address.

#### Rear-Foot Elevated Split Squat (Bulgarian Split Squat)
The rear leg's hip flexor is under stretch throughout the movement. Going deep (rear knee to or near the floor) maximizes this.

**Key technique:**
- Rear foot elevated on a bench or box, laces down
- Front foot far enough forward that you can reach depth without excessively forward-leaning torso
- Lower rear knee toward the floor — feel the hip flexor of the rear leg stretching
- Pause at the bottom; don't rush through

#### Deficit Reverse Lunge
Step backward onto a deficit (rear foot lower, or front foot elevated on a plate). Increases the hip flexor stretch on the trailing leg beyond what a standard lunge achieves.

#### Couch Stretch Loaded Progression
Start with the couch stretch (rear knee on floor, foot against wall, upright torso) as a passive stretch, then progress to adding a light dumbbell and performing small hip extension repetitions at the bottom of the range.

---

## Hip Adductors (Inner Thigh / Groin)

**Lengthened position:** Hip abducted (leg out to the side) — opposite of the power position.

Adductors are a common site of tightness and a significant limiter for hip mobility in squatting patterns.

#### Copenhagen Adductor Exercise
A well-researched exercise for adductor strength at lengthened positions. Directly addresses groin injuries in athletes. [MODERATE]

**Technique (long version):**
- Side-lying on the floor; top foot on an elevated surface (bench, box)
- Lift the bottom leg up to meet the top leg — you're adducting against gravity
- The top leg hangs off the side of the bench to increase range
- Lower slowly to a long stretch at the bottom

#### Sumo Deadlift
The wide stance places adductors under significant load through hip abduction range. An accessible way to build adductor strength if Copenhagen exercises are not yet available.

#### Side-Lying Hip Abduction (with resistance)
Load the abduction range using a cable, band, or ankle weight. The adductor is maximally stretched at the top of the range of motion.

#### Loaded Pancake / Straddle
Seated straddle with progressive forward lean, eventually adding load (plate on back) to increase the stretch stimulus in the adductors.

---

## Lats (Latissimus Dorsi)

**Lengthened position:** Arm overhead and slightly in front of the body — the reach position. Most lat training (pulldowns, rows) starts from this position but never emphasizes it.

Tight lats are a major limiter for overhead pressing, overhead reach, and thoracic extension.

#### Dead Hang
Hanging from a bar with full bodyweight provides a loaded stretch to the lats, teres major, and the entire shoulder girdle.

**Protocol:**
- Full passive hang, shoulders relaxed up toward ears
- 20–60 seconds per set; build gradually
- Active hang variation: slightly depress and retract scapulae to add co-contraction

**Why it matters:** Decompresses the spine, builds shoulder girdle capacity, and improves overhead range through consistent loaded stretch. [MODERATE]

#### Straight-Arm Pulldown
The straight-arm position removes elbow flexion from the equation and isolates the lat through its full overhead-to-side arc.

**Technique:**
- Cable or band set high; arms straight throughout
- Start with arms fully overhead (lat fully stretched) — this is the key position
- Pull down to hips while keeping arms straight
- Control the return to full overhead slowly

This is one of the best exercises for training the lat specifically in its lengthened position.

#### Overhead Lat Stretch Under Load
Standing cable or band overhead lat stretch — one arm at a time, pull laterally with light load while maintaining full overhead position. Builds active range in the overhead/lateral reach position.

---

## Chest (Pectorals)

**Lengthened position:** Arm extended out to the side and slightly behind the body — the "open chest" position at the bottom of a fly.

Most pressing movements do not load the pectorals at full stretch because the elbows are constrained.

#### Dumbbell Fly (with full stretch)
Unlike a cable fly, dumbbells allow the arm to travel behind the torso slightly at the bottom, increasing the stretch on the pectoral.

**Technique:**
- Wide arc from the top, lower dumbbells with a slight bend in the elbow
- Allow the weight to carry the arm into a full stretch at the bottom — feel the chest opening
- Do not bounce at the bottom; hold the stretch position for 1–2 seconds
- Use lighter weight than pressing — this is not a strength-focused movement in the same way

#### Cable Fly (low-to-high or same height)
A cable maintains constant tension throughout the range, including at full stretch (where a dumbbell tension drops off). The stretched position at maximum reach is the key training stimulus.

#### Incline Dumbbell Press
The incline angle shifts the pectoral stretch to a slightly different fiber orientation (upper pec) and allows a slightly larger range of motion at the bottom than flat bench.

---

## Shoulders (Deltoids)

### Rear Deltoid / Posterior Shoulder

**Lengthened position:** Arm across the body, internally rotated — the opposite of the shoulder's strong position.

#### Prone Y / T Raises
Lying face down, raise the arms overhead into a Y shape (for lower traps and rear deltoid) or out to the sides in a T. Load with very light dumbbells.

**The Y position** (arms reaching overhead at an angle) loads the rear deltoid and lower trapezius at full stretch and is one of the most underused movements for posterior shoulder health.

#### Cable Face Pull
Pull a rope attachment toward the face with elbows high. The start position (arms extended forward) is where the posterior shoulder is at its most stretched.

**The key:** Don't rush past the start position. Pause with arms extended, feeling the rear deltoid and external rotators under stretch.

### Lateral Deltoid

**Lengthened position:** Arm hanging at the side.

#### Cable Lateral Raise (from crossed-body position)
Set the cable at hip height on the opposite side of the body. Start with the arm crossing in front of the torso — this places the lateral deltoid under stretch. Raise the arm to 90 degrees.

The crossed-body start position is the critical difference from a standard lateral raise, which begins at the neutral (not lengthened) position.

---

## Biceps

**Lengthened position:** Arm fully extended, shoulder flexed (arm back behind the body) — this combination stretches the biceps across both joints it crosses.

#### Incline Dumbbell Curl
Lying back on a 45–60 degree incline bench. The arm hangs back behind the torso, which creates a full bicep stretch at the bottom of the movement.

**Why it's superior for length:** A standard standing curl begins with the arm at the side — already a partial stretch at best. The incline position puts the shoulder into extension, fully lengthening the long head of the bicep.

**Technique:**
- Full dead hang at the bottom — resist the urge to keep tension in the bicep at the start
- Curl from a dead stretch; feel the full range

#### Cable Curl with Arm Behind the Body
A cable set low, with the arm pulled behind the hip at the start. Creates a similar shoulder-extended, elbow-extended stretch.

---

## Triceps

**Lengthened position:** Elbow bent, arm overhead — the position at the bottom of a skull crusher or overhead extension.

The long head of the triceps crosses the shoulder joint. It is only fully stretched when the shoulder is flexed (arm overhead) AND the elbow is bent.

#### Overhead Tricep Extension (Cable or Dumbbell)
The long head is maximally loaded when the arm is overhead and the elbow is bent toward the back.

**Technique:**
- Seated or standing, single-arm or both arms
- Allow the dumbbell to lower behind the head into a full stretch — elbow pointing toward the ceiling
- The bottom position (full elbow flexion, arm overhead) is the target stretch
- Extend to full lockout

**The overhead position is critical.** A pushdown or close-grip bench press does not train the tricep at full length because the shoulder is not flexed.

---

## Upper Back (Rhomboids, Mid/Lower Traps, Rear Deltoid)

**Lengthened position:** Scapulae protracted (reaching forward) — the stretched rowing position.

#### Seated Cable Row with Full Reach
Most people stop the row at the neutral position and pull back. The **full forward reach at the start** — scapulae protracted, back rounded slightly — is where the mid-back musculature is fully lengthened.

**Technique:**
- At the start position, actively reach forward until you feel the mid-back stretch
- Hold the stretch for one second before pulling
- This transforms the standard row into a full-range exercise

#### Single-Arm Dumbbell Row with Stretch
Allow the dumbbell to lower into a full, shoulder-forward reach at the bottom. This is more range than most people use but directly trains the upper back at full length.

---

## Calves

**Lengthened position:** Ankle dorsiflexed (foot pulled toward shin) with knee slightly bent for soleus, or knee straight for gastrocnemius.

Tight calves are a significant limiter for squat depth, ankle mobility, and running mechanics.

#### Deficit Calf Raise
Stand with the ball of the foot on an elevated surface (step or plate) so the heel can drop well below parallel.

**The key:** Lower to the maximum stretch point — heel dropped as far as possible — before raising. Hold the bottom position for 1–2 seconds.

**Gastrocnemius:** Knee straight
**Soleus:** Knee bent (seated calf raise or bent-knee deficit raise)

Both should be trained — they are separate muscles with different leverage and different mobility contributions.

#### Tibialis Anterior (Shin) — Often Overlooked
The opposing muscle to the calf. Weakness here allows the calf to dominate and contributes to ankle instability and shin splints.

**Tibialis raise:** Stand with heels on an elevated surface; lower and raise the toes. Progress with resistance.

## Foam Roller: Lengthened Position Work

A long foam roller creates a fulcrum point that gravity works against — the roller elevates part of your body while everything around it is free to drop, passively loading muscles into their lengthened position. These exercises require no bands, no machines, and no gym.

Hold each position 30–60 seconds for passive stretch benefit, or perform 8–12 slow repetitions for an active loading effect.

---

### Thoracic Extension + Lat / Chest Opener

**Target:** Thoracic extensors (lengthened), chest, lats, anterior shoulder

**Position 1 — Segmental thoracic extension:**
- Roller placed horizontally across the mid-back (perpendicular to the spine)
- Hands behind head or arms crossed over chest
- Allow the upper back to drape over the roller into extension
- Move the roller 1–2 inches up the spine and repeat — work from mid-back to just below the neck
- The roller acts as a fulcrum; the chest opens and thoracic extensors lengthen as you fall over it

**Position 2 — Lat and chest opener:**
- Roller placed vertically *along* the spine (parallel, from tailbone to head)
- Lie back on the roller so the full spine is supported on it
- Spread arms out to the sides at shoulder height, palms up
- Let gravity pull the arms toward the floor — feel the chest and anterior shoulders opening
- For a lat bias: extend one arm fully overhead; let it fall toward the floor behind you

This is one of the most effective passive thoracic mobility tools available. The vertical roller position opens the chest in a way difficult to achieve any other way.

---

### Hip Flexor Extension

**Target:** Hip flexors, anterior hip capsule

- Roller placed horizontally under the sacrum / low back
- Extend one or both legs long
- The roller tilts the pelvis into posterior tilt, placing the hip flexors under a passive stretch in the extended leg position
- To increase: let one knee fall out to the side (frog position with roller support) or bring both legs long and breathe into the anterior hip

For a more active variation: from the roller-supported position, slowly lift one leg toward the ceiling and lower — the hip flexor of the lowering leg works eccentrically into the lengthened position.

---

### Prone Quad Stretch

**Target:** Quadriceps, especially rectus femoris

- Lie face down (prone), roller placed horizontally under one thigh
- Bend the knee toward the glutes as far as comfortable
- The roller elevates the thigh, amplifying the hip extension component and stretching the rectus femoris more than a standard prone quad stretch
- Add an active component: slowly extend and re-bend the knee for repetitions against the stretch

---

### Supported Hamstring / Posterior Chain Stretch

**Target:** Hamstrings, calves, spinal extensors

- Seated on the floor, roller under both thighs (just above the knees)
- Legs extended, feet flexed
- Hinge forward from the hip — reach toward the feet; the roller under the thighs supports the legs off the floor and slightly elevates them, increasing the hamstring stretch
- Hold and breathe; use gentle forward pulses to progressively increase range

---

### Side-Lying Pec / Anterior Shoulder Stretch

**Target:** Pectorals, anterior deltoid, bicipital tendon

- Lie on your side, roller under the ribcage (horizontally, supporting the side body)
- Bottom arm extended along the floor
- Top arm: rotate open toward the ceiling, then allow it to fall behind the body toward the floor
- The roller elevates the ribcage, giving the top arm more room to rotate behind you — amplifying the chest and anterior shoulder stretch at full external rotation
- Hold 30–60 seconds per side; breathe deeply into the stretch

---

### Roller-Supported Thoracic Rotation

**Target:** Thoracic rotators, obliques

- Lie on your back, knees bent, feet on floor
- Roller placed vertically along the spine
- Arms out to the sides (T position) for stability
- Let both knees fall to one side — feel the thoracic spine rotating open toward the opposite side
- The roller prevents the spine from collapsing fully flat, maintaining some thoracic extension while rotating
- Move slowly; pause at end range 3–5 seconds each side

---

### Calf and Plantar Fascia Stretch (Loaded)

**Target:** Gastrocnemius, soleus, plantar fascia

- Seated, roller under the calf of one leg; other leg crossed on top to increase pressure
- Actively dorsiflex the foot (pull toes toward shin) while keeping the heel in contact with the roller
- The roller provides sustained pressure while the dorsiflexion motion loads the calf at its lengthened position
- Work slowly from the upper calf toward the heel, holding 20–30 seconds at each position

---

## Resistance Bands: Lengthened Position Work

Bands are uniquely useful because they can be anchored at any height to create loading angles impossible with free weights. They also provide *assistance* at the hardest positions (where you're weakest and most stretched), making otherwise inaccessible exercises learnable.

A standard long resistance band and a door anchor (or any fixed object) cover everything here.

---

### Hamstrings

#### Band RDL
- Stand on the band, hip-width, holding both ends at hip height
- Hinge forward, pushing hips back — the band stretches, increasing tension as you reach the bottom
- Full stretch at the bottom, slow eccentric; same mechanics as dumbbell RDL with no gym required

#### Band Good Morning
- Step on the band; bring both ends over the shoulders and behind the neck (like a barbell good morning)
- Hip hinge forward to full hamstring stretch, then drive hips forward to return
- The band increases resistance as you return to upright — loads the concentric phase but the stretch at the bottom is what matters

#### Band-Assisted Nordic Curl
For those who cannot yet do a full Nordic curl — the band makes it accessible.
- Anchor a heavy band at hip height behind you; loop it around your torso
- The band provides assistance at the most stretched position (fully extended), where you are weakest
- As you get stronger, use progressively lighter bands until you no longer need assistance

---

### Hip Flexors

#### Half-Kneeling Band Hip Flexor Extension
One of the best active loaded hip flexor stretches.
- Anchor band low (floor level or door anchor at the bottom)
- Loop band around the rear thigh / hip crease
- Get into a half-kneeling position (rear knee on floor, front foot forward)
- The band pulls the rear hip forward into flexion — you work to extend it back, loading the hip flexor eccentrically into its lengthened position
- Hold the extended position 2–3 seconds before releasing; perform 10–12 repetitions per side

#### Band-Resisted Hip Flexor Stretch
Kneeling with band creating anterior pull on the rear hip:
- Anchor band at a forward point (door, rack)
- Loop around the rear hip; the band resists hip extension
- Drive hips forward into extension against the resistance — turns a passive hip flexor stretch into an active movement against load

---

### Glutes

#### Band Pull-Through
- Anchor band low behind you; reach between your legs to grab the band
- Hip hinge forward (band stretches and loads the hip flexion/glute stretch position)
- Drive hips forward powerfully to extend — glute and hamstring in their lengthened position at the bottom

#### Banded Hip Circle / Monster Walk
- Band around ankles; walk in a large circle maintaining tension throughout
- Trains hip abductors and adductors through their available range under constant resistance

---

### Adductors

#### Side-Lying Band Hip Adduction (Lengthened)
- Anchor band at ankle height to a fixed point; loop around the top ankle while side-lying
- Abduct the top leg against the band (adductor reaches its lengthened position)
- Hold end range 2–3 seconds; return slowly
- The adductor is under stretch at maximum abduction; the band loads that position

#### Standing Band Adduction
- Band anchored at ankle height to the side; loop around the outer ankle
- Stand on one leg; pull the banded leg inward across the midline against resistance
- The lengthened position is at maximum abduction at the start

---

### Lats

#### Band Straight-Arm Pulldown
- Anchor band at full overhead height
- Hold band with straight arms, step back to create tension
- Pull down to hips with arms straight — arms at full overhead (lat fully lengthened) is the start
- The key: pause with arms fully overhead before each rep; that's the position being trained

#### Band Overhead Lat Stretch (Loaded)
- Loop wrist through a band anchored overhead
- Step away so the band holds the arm up and creates traction
- Lean sideways and forward to load the lat at its most overhead, lengthened position
- Active version: with band holding arm overhead, perform small lateral bends against the tension

---

### Chest / Pectorals

#### Band Fly
- Anchor two bands at chest height on opposite sides (door anchors or two fixed points)
- Stand in the middle; arms open wide, slight elbow bend
- The stretched position (arms wide, open chest) is where the pec is maximally lengthened
- Bring hands together in front of you, hold, return slowly to the stretched position
- Unlike dumbbell flies, the band maintains tension at the stretched position (dumbbells lose it)

#### Supine Band Chest Stretch
- Anchor band overhead; lie on your back with the anchor above your head
- Hold band in one hand; let the arm fall out to the side and slightly behind
- The band creates a gentle loaded stretch on the pec and anterior shoulder at full external rotation
- Hold 30–60 seconds; breathe into the stretch

---

### Rear Deltoid / Upper Back

#### Band Pull-Apart
- Hold band with both hands at shoulder width, arms extended forward at shoulder height
- Pull the band apart horizontally until it touches the chest — the rear deltoid and rhomboids are maximally loaded at the end range (arms wide)
- The start position (arms forward) is where the rear deltoid is most lengthened
- Slow eccentric return is the key — control the band back to the starting stretch position
- Variation: overhead pull-apart (arms overhead) for a different angle

#### Band Face Pull
- Anchor band at face height; hold with both hands, palms down
- Pull toward the face with elbows high and flaring wide
- At the start (arms extended forward), the rear deltoid and external rotators are at their most stretched — pause here each rep
- Finish with hands at face level, elbows high

#### Band Row with Full Reach
- Anchor band at torso height; hold both ends
- **Actively reach as far forward as possible** before each pull — protract the scapulae fully
- This loaded stretch position is the target; the row itself simply returns you to neutral
- The band creates constant tension through the full range including the forward-reach position that a dumbbell row (relying on gravity) cannot replicate

---

### Shoulders

#### Band Dislocates (Shoulder Pass-Through)
The most complete shoulder mobility exercise for full-arc range.
- Hold a long band with a wide grip (hands as wide as needed to complete the movement)
- With straight arms, bring the band from in front of the body — overhead — behind the body, touching the glutes — and back
- The band is forgiving and adjustable: widen grip if range is limited; narrow it progressively over weeks
- This moves the shoulder through its full arc in a controlled, loaded way, including the overhead lengthened position, the behind-the-back position, and everything in between

#### Band Lateral Raise (Lengthened Start)
- Stand on the band; hold the opposite end so the band crosses under the opposite foot — this makes the arm start across the body (adducted past midline)
- Raise the arm out to the side against resistance
- Starting position (arm across body) is where the lateral deltoid is most lengthened

---

### Biceps

#### Band Curl with Arm Behind the Body
- Anchor band low behind you; step forward so the band pulls the arm back into extension
- The starting position — arm extended and pulled behind the hip — is the lengthened bicep position (shoulder extended, elbow extended simultaneously)
- Curl from this stretched position; return slowly to the full stretch

---

### Triceps

#### Band Overhead Tricep Extension
- Anchor band low or behind the body
- Reach overhead, hold the band with elbow bent — arm overhead, elbow pointing toward the ceiling
- The bottom/start position (elbow maximally bent, arm overhead) is full tricep stretch
- Extend to lockout; return slowly to the stretched position
- The band's resistance increases as you extend, which matches the strength curve of the tricep (stronger at lockout)

---

### Calves and Tibialis

#### Band Dorsiflexion (Tibialis + Calf Stretch)
- Sit on the floor, loop band around the foot (across the mid-foot)
- Anchor band forward (or hold it); dorsiflex the foot (pull toes toward shin) against the band's resistance
- This directly strengthens tibialis anterior and simultaneously loads the calf in its most lengthened position (full dorsiflexion)
- 15–20 reps per set; control the return (calf eccentrically loads back to plantarflexion)

---

## Putting It Together

You don't need to do every exercise on this list. Prioritize based on where you are most restricted:

| If you lack... | Gym | Foam Roller | Resistance Band |
|---|---|---|---|
| Squat depth | ATG squat, deficit calf raise | Prone quad stretch | Band dorsiflexion |
| Hamstring flexibility | RDL with slow eccentric, Nordic curl | Supported hamstring stretch | Band RDL, band-assisted Nordic |
| Hip flexor mobility | Bulgarian split squat, deficit reverse lunge | Hip flexor extension on roller | Half-kneeling band hip flexor extension |
| Overhead reach | Dead hang, straight-arm pulldown | Thoracic extension + lat opener | Band straight-arm pulldown, band dislocates |
| Chest / anterior shoulder | Dumbbell fly, incline DB press | Side-lying pec stretch on roller | Band fly, supine band chest stretch |
| Hip adductor mobility | Copenhagen adductor, sumo deadlift | — | Side-lying band adduction, standing band adduction |
| Rear shoulder / upper back | Cable row with full reach | — | Band pull-apart, band face pull, band row with reach |
| Thoracic stiffness | — | Segmental thoracic extension | Band dislocates (overhead arc) |
| General posterior chain length | Jefferson curl (when ready), good mornings | Thoracic extension on roller | Band good morning |

**A reasonable starting protocol:**

Pick 2–3 of these exercises per session, selected for your most restricted areas. Treat them like strength training sets (3–4 sets of 8–12 reps with a controlled eccentric) rather than stretching.

The principle is consistent across all of them: **slow down at the lengthened position, load it progressively, and build strength where your body currently treats as the edge of control.** That edge will move outward over time.
