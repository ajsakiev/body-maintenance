# Daily Mobility Routine

A practical, Pilates-compatible mobility plan that adds 10–20 minutes of targeted joint work to your existing practice. Every exercise here links to its detailed source page for full technique instructions.

> **Design principle:** This routine fills the gaps in Pilates — specifically hip end-range loading, ankle dorsiflexion, and dedicated thoracic rotation. It is not a replacement for Pilates; it is the complement that makes your Pilates practice more effective over time.

---

## How to Use This Plan

**Two modes:**
- **Full session (15–20 min):** When you have time — follow the AM sequence below
- **Minimum viable (5 min):** The emergency version for busy days — starred items only ★

**Weekly rotation** keeps volume manageable and prevents overdoing any one area:

| Day | Focus Area | AM Sequence | PM Add-on |
|-----|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| Mon | Hips | Hip CARs ★ + 90/90 PAILs/RAILs + Hip flexor | Pigeon stretch |
| Tue | Thoracic | Cat-cow ★ + Foam roller + Open book | Rib breathing |
| Wed | Shoulders | Shoulder CARs ★ + Wall slide + Pec stretch | Sleeper stretch |
| Thu | Hips + Ankles | Hip CARs ★ + Banded ankle + Deep squat hold | Frog stretch |
| Fri | Full sequence | All CARs ★ + Priority areas | Thread the needle |
| Sat | Thoracic + Shoulders | Foam roller + Open book + Shoulder CARs ★ | — |
| Sun | Recovery | Light walk or foam rolling only | — |

## The AM Sequence (15–20 min)

Do this before or after Pilates. Before Pilates primes the joints for fuller range. After Pilates deepens end-range work when the tissues are warm.

### Step 1: Joint CARs (3–4 min) ★

*Always start here. Warms up synovial fluid and takes every joint through its full range before loading.*

| Joint | Duration |
|-------|----------|
| Neck CARs (slow, full circles) | 3 circles each direction |
| Shoulder CARs | 3–5 circles each direction, each side |
| Hip CARs | 3–5 circles each direction, each side |
| Ankle CARs | 5 circles each direction, each foot |

Keep movements slow and controlled — the point is full-range active control, not speed.

---

### Step 2: Thoracic (4 min)

**Cat-cow with segmental focus — 2 min**
Slow, 10 reps. Find the stiff segments and pause on them.

**Open book — 90 sec/side**
8 reps each side. Keep knees stacked.

---

### Step 3: Hips (6 min)

**90/90 PAILs/RAILs — 3 min/side**
2 min passive settling → PAILs 10 sec → RAILs 10 sec → active push deeper. This is the single highest-value exercise in the plan.

**Hip flexor stretch — 60 sec/side**
Kneeling lunge. Posterior tilt the pelvis. Glute squeeze add-on.

---

### Step 4: Ankles (3 min)

**Banded dorsiflexion mobilization — 90 sec/side**
15–20 knee-forward reps with the band. Or substitute wall test knee drives if no band.

**Deep squat hold — 60–90 sec**
Hold at the bottom, pry with elbows.

---

## The PM Wind-Down (8–10 min)

Evening work uses longer passive holds — tissues respond differently when they're warmer and less guarded.

**Pigeon stretch — 2–3 min/side**
Let gravity do the work. The longer you hold, the better.

**Doorway pec stretch — 90 sec/side**
With PAILs: contract for 10 sec, release, step deeper.

**Thoracic foam roller — 3 min**
Work each segment, 30–45 sec each. Breathe into each position.

**Soleus / calf stretch — 60 sec/side**
Bent-knee version for soleus. Against a wall.

---

## The 5-Minute Emergency Sequence ★

When you have almost no time. These five movements cover the most important joints and take exactly 5 minutes:

| Exercise | Time |
|----------|------|
| Shoulder CARs (both sides) | 60 sec |
| Hip CARs (both sides) | 60 sec |
| 90/90 hip hold (both sides) | 60 sec |
| Thoracic foam roller (mid-back only) | 60 sec |
| Deep squat hold | 60 sec |

**Total: 5 minutes.** Do this rather than nothing. Consistency over perfection.

## Progress Benchmarks

Use these tests monthly to track objective improvement:

| Test | Starting point | 3-month target |
|------|---------------|----------------|
| Wall ankle test (toes to wall) | Note your distance | Add 1–2 inches |
| Sit-and-rise test | Score /10 | Improve by 1–2 points |
| Overhead reach (wall test) | Can arms touch wall? | Yes, without arching |
| 90/90 hip: how far can you lean? | Note angle | Noticeably further |
| Thoracic rotation (seated) | Note range each side | +10–15° each side |

## Integration Tips for Pilates Practitioners

1. **Do CARs before Pilates** — 5 min of hip and shoulder CARs primes the joints and makes Pilates cues easier to access

2. **Hip internal rotation is the gap** — Pilates rarely externally cues hip internal rotation. The 90/90 work addresses this directly.

3. **Thoracic work makes Pilates articulations better** — The spine articulations in roll-up, rolling like a ball, and spinal massage all improve when thoracic segmental mobility is higher.

4. **Ankle work improves footwork** — More dorsiflexion means fuller range and better alignment in Reformer footwork and standing exercises.

5. **Don't stretch before high-intensity Pilates** — Static stretching (pigeon, pec) before any high-intensity Pilates work temporarily reduces muscle output. Save long passive holds for after.

> **Realistic timeline:** With consistent daily practice, expect to feel a meaningful difference in 3–4 weeks. Measurable (testable) improvement in all areas within 8–12 weeks. Mobility training is cumulative — it rewards consistency more than intensity.
