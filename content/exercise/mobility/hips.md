# Hip Mobility

The hip is the most important joint for movement quality. It sits at the center of the kinetic chain — restriction here forces compensation upstream (lower back) and downstream (knees). Most adults have significant restriction in hip internal rotation and hip flexion from prolonged sitting.

> **Pilates note:** Pilates trains hip stability and alignment but rarely takes hips through full end-range active control in all planes. These exercises specifically target the ranges Pilates leaves undertrained: deep internal rotation, end-range flexion, and loaded hip extension.

---

## Self-Assessment

Before working, know where your restrictions are:

**Hip Internal Rotation Test**
Sit on the edge of a table, legs hanging. Let one foot swing outward. How far does it go?
- Normal: foot moves 40–45° outward (which equals 40–45° internal rotation)
- Restricted: < 30° — common; priority target
- Asymmetry between sides is also important to note

**Hip Flexion Depth**
Stand and bring one knee to chest with hands. Can your thigh reach parallel to the floor without your pelvis tucking?
- If your lower back rounds to get your knee up, your hip flexion is restricted

**Sit-and-Rise Test**
From standing, lower to seated cross-legged on the floor, then rise without using your hands or knees.
- Score /10 (–1 for each hand, knee, or forearm used; –0.5 for a balance touch)
- Score < 8 is associated with significantly higher mortality risk [MODERATE]

## Exercises

### 1. 90/90 Hip Stretch with PAILs/RAILs

**What it targets:** Hip internal and external rotation simultaneously  
**Time:** 3–5 min/side  
**When:** Daily if possible; morning or pre-Pilates

**Setup:** Sit with front shin parallel to your body (90° at knee), rear shin parallel behind you (also 90°). Sit as tall as possible.

**Passive phase (2 min):**
Sink into the position. Let gravity do the work. Breathe into the stretch without forcing.

**PAILs (Progressive Angular Isometric Loading):**
Press your front knee down into the floor as hard as you can without moving. 20–60% effort for 10 seconds. Breathe out during the contraction.

**RAILs (Regressive Angular Isometric Loading):**
Now actively try to lift your front shin off the floor (it won't move). 20–40% effort for 10 seconds.

**Active reach:**
Relax both contractions. Now use your own muscles to push further into the stretch. Hold the new end-range for 5–10 seconds.

**2–3 sets/side. Switch sides.**

> This is the single most effective hip mobility exercise. The PAILs/RAILs cycle uses isometric loading to neurologically "accept" the new range — producing lasting change rather than temporary elongation. [MODERATE]

### 2. Hip CARs (Controlled Articular Rotations)

**What it targets:** Full joint circumduction; neurological ownership of the hip joint  
**Time:** 1 min/side  
**When:** Every morning as a "joint health" practice

**How to do it:**
1. Stand on one leg, holding a wall for balance
2. Bring the free knee up to hip height
3. Slowly rotate the hip in the largest circle you can make — forward, out to the side, back behind you, and return
4. Keep the spine completely still throughout; all motion is at the hip joint
5. 3–5 reps each direction, each side

**What to feel:** A warm, working sensation deep in the hip socket. This is not a stretch — it is active joint loading at every angle.

> Hip CARs are a central practice of Functional Range Conditioning (FRC). Daily practice maintains joint health, synovial fluid distribution, and the neurological map of the joint. [MODERATE]

### 3. Deep Squat / Hip Flexion Hold

**What it targets:** Hip flexion depth, ankle dorsiflexion, thoracic mobility  
**Time:** 1–3 min total  
**When:** Morning; between tasks (literally just squat for 1 min)

**How to do it:**
1. Feet shoulder-width, toes turned out 20–30°
2. Squat as deep as possible, keeping heels on the floor
3. Hold onto a doorframe, TRX, or banister if needed to keep heels down
4. "Pry" — once at the bottom, push knees outward with elbows while clasping hands

**Progression:** Work toward holding a full squat without support, heels flat, for 2–3 minutes.

**If heels rise:** Ankle restriction is the limiter — see Ankles page. Place a folded towel under heels temporarily while building dorsiflexion.

### 4. Hip Flexor Stretch — Kneeling (with Active Posterior Tilt)

**What it targets:** Hip flexors (psoas, rectus femoris), restricted by prolonged sitting  
**Time:** 60–90 sec/side  
**When:** After sitting, post-Pilates, evening

**How to do it:**
1. Kneeling lunge position — right knee on floor, left foot forward
2. Posteriorly tilt the pelvis (tuck tailbone under; flatten lower back) — this is the key step most people skip
3. Lean slightly forward / shift hips forward until you feel a stretch at the front of the right hip
4. Hold 60 sec — the stretch should intensify at 30–45 sec as the nervous system settles
5. **Active add-on:** Squeeze the rear glute hard for 10 sec (activates antagonist; deepens stretch). Release. Try to push deeper.

**What to feel:** Stretching at the front of the hip/groin, not the knee. If you feel it in the knee, adjust your position.

---

### 5. Pigeon Stretch

**What it targets:** External hip rotators (piriformis, glute medius), often very tight  
**Time:** 2–3 min/side  
**When:** Post-workout, evening, after long periods of sitting

**How to do it:**
1. From all fours, bring one shin forward and lay it on the ground in front of you (ideally parallel to the front of your mat, working toward that over time)
2. Extend the rear leg straight behind
3. Sink your hips toward the floor
4. Walk hands forward to increase the stretch; rest forehead on hands or a block
5. Hold 2–3 min/side — longer holds are more effective here

**Modification:** If this is too intense, do a supine figure-4 stretch (lying on back, cross ankle over opposite knee, pull both knees toward chest).

---

### 6. Frog Stretch

**What it targets:** Hip adductors, groin, inner thigh — often the limiting factor for deep squat depth  
**Time:** 2–3 min  
**When:** Evening or after cardio

**How to do it:**
1. On all fours, slide knees wide until you feel a stretch in the groin
2. Feet turned out in line with knees
3. Sink hips back toward heels as far as possible
4. Rock gently forward and back to find the peak of the stretch

**Active add-on (PAILs):** Try to squeeze your knees together (adduction) against the floor for 10 seconds. Release. Sink further.

## Weekly Prescription

| Priority | Exercise | Duration | When |
|---------|----------|----------|------|
| Daily | Hip CARs | 2 min | Morning |
| Daily | 90/90 + PAILs/RAILs | 6–10 min | Morning or pre-Pilates |
| 3–4×/week | Hip flexor stretch | 3 min | After sitting |
| 3–4×/week | Pigeon stretch | 6 min | Evening |
| 2–3×/week | Deep squat hold | 3 min | Any time |
| 2×/week | Frog stretch | 3 min | Evening |

> Total daily commitment: 8–15 minutes. Hip mobility shows measurable improvement within 4–6 weeks of consistent daily practice. [MODERATE]
