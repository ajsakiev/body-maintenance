# Body Composition

Body composition — the ratio of lean mass (muscle, bone, organs) to fat mass — is a more accurate predictor of metabolic health and longevity than bodyweight or BMI alone. Building and maintaining lean mass while keeping adiposity low is the target, not a specific number on a scale.

---

## Why Body Composition Matters More Than Weight

**BMI is a poor individual predictor of health.** A 75kg person at 22% body fat has a very different metabolic profile than a 75kg person at 12% body fat — but their BMI is identical.

Better metrics:

| Metric | What It Measures | Notes |
|--------|-----------------|-------|
| Body fat % | Fat vs. lean mass ratio | DEXA scan is most accurate |
| Waist circumference | Abdominal (visceral) fat | >102cm men / >88cm women = elevated risk |
| Waist-to-height ratio | Central adiposity | Should be <0.5 (waist < half your height) |
| Muscle mass index | Lean mass relative to height | Strongest mortality predictor [STRONG] |
| DEXA scan | Total body composition | Gold standard; widely available |

**Visceral fat** (fat around abdominal organs) is metabolically active and pro-inflammatory — more dangerous than subcutaneous fat (under the skin). Visceral fat predicts metabolic disease, cardiovascular risk, and mortality better than total fat mass. [STRONG]

> [Source: Despres et al. (2012) — visceral fat and cardiometabolic risk](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22243842/)

## Protein: The Central Variable

Protein is the single most important macronutrient for body composition. It:
- Provides substrate for muscle protein synthesis [STRONG]
- Has the highest thermic effect of food (20–30% of calories burned in digestion) [STRONG]
- Produces the greatest satiety per calorie of any macronutrient [STRONG]
- Preserves lean mass during caloric restriction [STRONG]

### Protein Targets

| Goal | Daily Target |
|------|-------------|
| General health maintenance | 1.2–1.6 g/kg body weight |
| Building muscle | 1.6–2.2 g/kg |
| Fat loss while preserving muscle | 2.0–2.4 g/kg |
| Over 50 (anabolic resistance increases) | 2.0–2.4 g/kg minimum |
| Over 65 | 2.2–2.6 g/kg recommended |

> [Source: Morton et al. (2018) — upper limit of protein benefit for hypertrophy, meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/)

> [Source: Bauer et al. (2013) — protein for older adults, ESPEN recommendations](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23867520/)

### Protein Quality

Not all protein is equal. Amino acid profile, leucine content, and digestibility determine muscle protein synthesis effectiveness:

| Source | Leucine Content | Digestibility | Notes |
|--------|----------------|---------------|-------|
| Whey protein | High | ~100% | Fast-absorbing; ideal post-workout |
| Eggs | High | ~91% | Complete; excellent overall quality |
| Chicken/turkey | High | ~90–95% | Lean; practical daily staple |
| Beef | High | ~85–90% | Complete; also high in creatine |
| Salmon/fish | High | ~90% | Plus EPA/DHA omega-3s |
| Greek yogurt | Moderate-high | ~90% | Casein-heavy (slower release) |
| Lentils/legumes | Lower | ~70–80% | Incomplete; combine with grains |
| Soy protein | Moderate | ~84% | Most complete plant source |
| Pea protein | Moderate | ~75% | Second best plant source |

**Leucine threshold:** ~2.5–3g leucine per meal maximally stimulates muscle protein synthesis (MPS). This requires roughly 30–40g of most animal proteins, or larger portions of plant sources. [MODERATE]

### Protein Timing [MODERATE]

Spreading protein across 3–4 meals (rather than 1–2 large meals) maximizes repeated MPS pulses throughout the day:

- 30–50g protein per meal, every 3–5 hours
- Post-workout protein within 2 hours (the "anabolic window" is wider than once thought — but proximity to training is still beneficial for muscle recovery) [MODERATE]

> [Source: Areta et al. (2013) — protein distribution and MPS](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23459753/)

## Caloric Balance: The Foundation

Body composition change requires caloric manipulation. No dietary strategy overrides energy balance. [STRONG]

### Losing Fat

**Caloric deficit** is required for fat loss. The source of that deficit (less food, more exercise, or both) matters less than sustaining it.

| Deficit | Expected Fat Loss | Notes |
|---------|-----------------|-------|
| 250–300 kcal/day | ~0.2–0.3 kg/week | Slow; maximizes muscle preservation |
| 500 kcal/day | ~0.4–0.5 kg/week | Most common recommendation |
| 750–1000 kcal/day | ~0.7–1.0 kg/week | Higher risk of muscle loss; requires very high protein |

**Aggressive deficits (>750 kcal/day)** significantly increase muscle catabolism, even with high protein intake. A slower pace with higher protein is better for body composition outcomes. [STRONG]

**Muscle preservation during fat loss requires:**
1. High protein (2.0–2.4 g/kg) [STRONG]
2. Continued resistance training [STRONG]
3. Moderate deficit (not aggressive) [MODERATE]

> [Source: Helms et al. (2014) — evidence-based recommendations for natural bodybuilding competition prep](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24092765/)

### Building Muscle (Lean Bulk)

A small caloric surplus supports muscle growth without excess fat gain:

- **100–300 kcal/day above TDEE** (total daily energy expenditure) is sufficient for most people
- Expect ~0.2–0.5 kg total weight gain per month (mostly lean mass if training consistently)
- If gaining faster than 0.5 kg/month, reduce surplus — excess goes to fat

**Body recomposition** (simultaneously gaining muscle and losing fat) is achievable for beginners, those returning after a break, and those with significant fat mass to lose — even in a slight deficit. [MODERATE]

## Carbohydrates & Fat: The Other Macros

Once protein is set, fat and carbohydrates can be flexed based on preference and context.

### Carbohydrates

**Carbohydrates are not uniquely fattening** — calories are. However, carbohydrate quality matters enormously for metabolic health. [STRONG]

| Type | Impact | Examples |
|------|--------|---------|
| High-fiber, low-glycemic | Positive | Legumes, vegetables, oats, berries |
| Starchy whole foods | Neutral-positive | Sweet potatoes, brown rice, quinoa |
| Refined/processed | Negative | White bread, pastries, sugary drinks |

**Carbohydrates around training:** Consuming carbohydrates near training sessions (before and/or after) supports performance and glycogen replenishment. This is more relevant for high-volume training than casual exercise. [MODERATE]

### Dietary Fat

Fat is not the enemy it was believed to be in the 1980s–90s. [STRONG]

**Prioritize:**
- Monounsaturated fats (EVOO, avocado, almonds)
- Omega-3 fatty acids (fatty fish, fish oil)
- Saturated fat from whole food sources (eggs, full-fat dairy, meat) — in moderation

**Minimize:**
- Trans fats (partially hydrogenated oils) — these are unambiguously harmful [STRONG]
- Excess omega-6 industrial seed oils (soybean, corn, sunflower)

**Fat and satiety:** Dietary fat is highly satiating and does not spike blood insulin. Very low fat diets reduce satiety and can make caloric adherence harder. [MODERATE]

## Practical Meal Architecture

A simple framework that hits protein targets, manages calories, and supports metabolic health:

**Each meal should contain:**
1. A protein anchor (30–50g) — chicken, fish, eggs, Greek yogurt, legumes + grain combo
2. Non-starchy vegetables (half the plate by volume)
3. A fat source — olive oil, avocado, nuts, or naturally fatty protein
4. Optional starch — earned by exercise; timed around training

**Beverage rule:** Calories in beverages are the easiest calories to reduce without noticing. Water, black coffee, and unsweetened tea should dominate. Juice, sweetened drinks, and alcohol all add significant calories with minimal satiety effect.

## Tracking: When and How

Tracking food intake for 2–4 weeks produces excellent awareness of typical intake even if you don't track long-term. [MODERATE]

**Apps:** Cronometer (more accurate nutrient data), MyFitnessPal (larger food database)

You don't have to track forever. A period of tracking teaches intuition — most people significantly underestimate caloric intake and overestimate protein intake before tracking.

**Body weight tracking:** Weigh daily and use a 7-day rolling average. Daily weight fluctuates 1–3kg based on water, food volume, and glycogen. The trend over 2–4 weeks is the signal.
