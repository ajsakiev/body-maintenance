# Longevity & Anti-Inflammatory Eating

Chronic low-grade inflammation is a driver of virtually every major aging-related disease — cardiovascular disease, cancer, Alzheimer's, type 2 diabetes, and arthritis. Dietary patterns are among the most powerful modulators of systemic inflammation, and the evidence here is unusually consistent across different study designs.

---

## The Inflammation-Aging Connection

**Inflammaging** — the term coined by geroscientist Claudio Franceschi — describes the chronic, sterile, low-grade inflammation that accumulates with age. It predicts biological age better than chronological age, and it is significantly modifiable through diet and lifestyle. [STRONG]

Diet quality scores (Mediterranean Diet Score, DASH score) are inversely correlated with:
- C-reactive protein (CRP) — primary inflammatory marker [STRONG]
- Interleukin-6 (IL-6) and TNF-α [STRONG]
- All-cause mortality over 10–20 year follow-ups [STRONG]

The strongest dietary pattern in the literature for both longevity and anti-inflammation is the **Mediterranean diet**.

---

## The Mediterranean Diet: The Gold Standard Evidence Base

No dietary pattern has more RCT evidence for cardiovascular and longevity outcomes than the Mediterranean diet. [STRONG]

> **PREDIMED Trial (2013, 7,447 participants):** High-risk adults assigned to a Mediterranean diet supplemented with olive oil or nuts had a **30% reduction in major cardiovascular events** compared to a low-fat diet control. The trial was stopped early because the benefit was so large it was considered unethical to withhold.
>
> [Source: Estruch et al. (2013, updated 2018)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23432189/)

## Key Anti-Inflammatory Foods & Compounds

### Extra Virgin Olive Oil (EVOO) [STRONG]

EVOO contains **oleocanthal** (a natural COX-1/COX-2 inhibitor with ibuprofen-like properties) and **hydroxytyrosol** (potent antioxidant). Studies show measurable reduction in CRP and IL-6 with regular EVOO consumption.

- Use for cooking (moderate heat) and raw dressings
- Look for cloudy, peppery-tasting oil — indicates high polyphenol content
- 3–4 tablespoons per day is the well-studied dose

### Omega-3 Fatty Acids (EPA & DHA) [STRONG]

Omega-3s downregulate pro-inflammatory eicosanoids and cytokines. The EPA:DHA ratio matters, and marine sources (fish, not plant flaxseed) are far superior in bioavailability. [STRONG]

**Best sources:**
- Fatty fish: salmon, sardines, mackerel, herring, anchovies
- Fish oil supplementation if intake is low (1–3g EPA+DHA/day)

> [Source: Calder (2017) — omega-3 and inflammation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28900017/)

**ALA (from flaxseed/walnuts) converts to EPA/DHA at only 5–10% efficiency** in humans — not a substitute for marine omega-3s. [STRONG]

### Polyphenols [MODERATE]

Plant polyphenols act as antioxidants and anti-inflammatory agents via multiple pathways including NF-κB suppression and SIRT1 activation.

High-polyphenol foods:
- **Berries** (blueberries, blackberries, strawberries)
- **Dark leafy greens** (spinach, kale, arugula)
- **Green tea** — EGCG is one of the most studied polyphenols
- **Dark chocolate** (>70% cocoa)
- **Red/purple vegetables** (red cabbage, beets, purple onion)
- **Turmeric** with black pepper (curcumin + piperine — piperine dramatically increases bioavailability) [MODERATE]

### Cruciferous Vegetables [STRONG]

Broccoli, cauliflower, Brussels sprouts, kale, and cabbage contain **sulforaphane** and **indole-3-carbinol** — compounds with well-documented anti-cancer and anti-inflammatory properties. Sulforaphane activates the Nrf2 pathway, which upregulates the body's own antioxidant defenses.

> [Source: Fahey et al. (1997) — sulforaphane, foundational research from Johns Hopkins](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9294217/)

Aim for cruciferous vegetables 4–5 times per week. Lightly steaming or eating raw preserves more sulforaphane than boiling.

## What to Reduce

### Ultra-Processed Foods [STRONG]

Ultra-processed food (UPF) — packaged products with ingredients you wouldn't find in a home kitchen — is strongly associated with inflammation, obesity, cardiovascular disease, and cancer.

> **NOVA classification:** UPFs are Group 4 — not just high in salt/sugar/fat, but industrially reformulated with emulsifiers, artificial flavors, colorings, and preservatives that appear to independently harm gut microbiome integrity and barrier function. [MODERATE]

> [Source: Monteiro et al. (2019) — NOVA classification and health outcomes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30744710/)

**Practical check:** If a product has more than 5 ingredients and contains items you don't recognize as food, it's likely ultra-processed.

### Refined Carbohydrates & Added Sugars [STRONG]

High glycemic foods drive postprandial blood glucose spikes, which trigger inflammatory cascades, advanced glycation end-products (AGEs), and oxidative stress. [STRONG]

- Replace white bread, white rice, pasta with whole grain versions
- Reduce added sugar intake (WHO recommends <10% of daily calories; <5% for additional benefit)
- Fruit contains fructose but also fiber, polyphenols, and water — the glycemic impact is far lower than processed sugar

### Industrial Seed Oils [MODERATE]

Soybean, corn, cottonseed, and sunflower oils contain high levels of omega-6 linoleic acid. While not directly harmful in modest amounts, extremely high omega-6:omega-3 ratios (modern Western diets often reach 15:1 or higher) may promote a pro-inflammatory state. [MODERATE]

Target omega-6:omega-3 ratio of 4:1 or lower. Practical approach: use EVOO as primary cooking fat; reduce ultra-processed food (primary source of seed oils).

## Longevity Dietary Patterns: Beyond Mediterranean

### Blue Zone Eating Patterns [MODERATE]

Five populations with extraordinary longevity (Sardinia, Okinawa, Loma Linda, Nicoya, Ikaria) share dietary commonalities:

- **Mostly plant-based** (not necessarily vegetarian, but 90–95% of diet is plants)
- **Legumes as primary protein** (beans, lentils, tofu in Okinawa)
- **Minimal processed food**
- **Moderate caloric intake** — Okinawans traditionally practice *Hara Hachi Bu* ("eat until 80% full")
- **Low red meat, minimal sugar**

> [Source: Buettner (2008), *The Blue Zones*; and Willcox et al. (2007) — Okinawan dietary patterns](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17986602/)

### Caloric Restriction & Longevity [MODERATE]

CR produces consistent lifespan extension in animal models and clear metabolic benefits in human studies. [MODERATE] The challenge is sustainability.

**CR mimetics** — compounds that activate similar pathways without caloric restriction — are an active area of research:
- **Metformin:** Activates AMPK, used off-label as a longevity drug [MODERATE]
- **Rapamycin (mTOR inhibitor):** Strongest longevity evidence in animal models; not yet studied for healthy human longevity [EMERGING]
- **Resveratrol:** Initial excitement faded; bioavailability issues in humans [MODERATE, modest]
- **Spermidine:** Found in fermented foods, legumes, mushrooms; activates autophagy [EMERGING]

> [Source: Fontana & Partridge (2015) — caloric restriction and longevity review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25720075/)

**Time-restricted eating (TRE):** Limiting eating to a 8–10 hour window aligns food intake with circadian biology. Metabolic benefits beyond simple caloric reduction are still debated. [MODERATE]

## Practical Summary

**Daily non-negotiables:**
1. Extra virgin olive oil as primary fat (3–4 tbsp)
2. 2–3 cups vegetables including leafy greens
3. Protein source at each meal (fish 2–3×/week)
4. Berries or other polyphenol-rich fruit
5. Water as primary beverage

**Weekly targets:**
- 3+ servings legumes
- 2–3 servings oily fish
- Daily nuts (small handful)
- Cruciferous vegetables 4–5×

**Reduce / eliminate:**
- Ultra-processed foods
- Refined grains
- Added sugar
- Processed meats (hot dogs, deli meat, bacon)
