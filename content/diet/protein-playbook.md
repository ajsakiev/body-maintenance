# Protein Playbook

A practical system for hitting a daily protein target on a messy schedule: fast food, spotty meals, snacks included. The goal here isn't a shredded physique. It's getting enough protein to recover from lifting and keep building muscle, while still eating like a normal person.

---

## Setting the Number

Protein benefits for muscle growth level off around **1.6 g per kg of bodyweight per day**, with an upper confidence limit near 2.2 g/kg. Above that, extra protein does nothing measurable for muscle. [STRONG]

> [Source: Morton et al. (2018) — protein supplementation and resistance training, meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/)

| Level | Per kg | Per lb | Use it when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Floor | 1.2 g/kg | 0.55 g/lb | Maintaining muscle, not lifting much |
| **Target** | **1.6 g/kg** | **~0.7 g/lb** | Lifting regularly and building muscle; gets essentially all the benefit |
| Ceiling | 2.2 g/kg | 1.0 g/lb | Hard training while dieting; no benefit beyond this |

### Worked Example: 225 lb (102 kg), Lifting 2–3×/Week

| | Grams/day |
|---|---|
| Floor | ~125 g |
| **Daily target** | **~160 g** |
| Ceiling | ~225 g |

**160 g is the number.** Weeks averaging 140–150 g still capture most of the benefit. Consistency in the right range beats precision.

---

## Does Getting Leaner Lower the Target?

Somewhat, and eventually. Protein needs track **lean mass**, not fat mass, so losing fat while keeping muscle lowers the target over time. A common shortcut is to eat **0.7–1 g per lb of goal bodyweight** rather than current bodyweight.

The catch: **during a calorie deficit, protein needs go up, not down.** Protein is what keeps a deficit from eating into muscle, and higher intakes (toward 2.2+ g/kg) are best supported for lean, trained people who are cutting. [MODERATE]

> [Source: Helms et al. (2014) — protein during caloric restriction in resistance-trained athletes, systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=Helms+2014+dietary+protein+caloric+restriction+resistance+trained+lean+athletes)

Deliberately losing muscle to reduce protein needs is a poor trade. Muscle mass is one of the strongest predictors of healthy aging. See [Body Composition](content/diet/body-composition.md).

## Distribution: Chunks, Not One Big Dinner

Muscle protein synthesis responds best to several moderate doses spread across the day, roughly **0.4 g/kg per meal across at least 4 meals**. For a 102 kg person that works out to about **40 g per sitting**. [MODERATE]

> [Source: Schoenfeld & Aragon (2018) — how much protein can the body use in a single meal](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=Schoenfeld+Aragon+2018+how+much+protein+single+meal+muscle-building)

One large protein dinner still counts toward the daily total, since daily intake matters most. But four ~40 g hits are easier to reach and slightly better for recovery. Getting one of them within a couple of hours of lifting helps with early-training soreness. See [DOMS & Soreness](content/exercise/recovery/doms-soreness.md).

## The Anchor System

Don't design perfect meals. Set up a few **protein anchors** that happen every day no matter how chaotic the day is.

| Anchor | Protein | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Shake #1** (morning) | ~40–50 g | Two scoops of whey. Replaces a skipped or random breakfast. |
| **Shake #2** (post-lift or mid-afternoon) | ~40–50 g | Covers the gap before dinner. |
| **One protein-built meal** | ~40–60 g | Anything, fast food included, as long as it's built around a big protein portion. |
| **Snack swaps** | ~20–40 g | Keep snacking, just shift some of it toward protein. |

Two shakes cover **more than half of a 160 g target** before any food decisions.

### Build It One Habit at a Time

| Weeks | Add | Daily check |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Shake #1, every morning | Shake? ✓ |
| 3–4 | One protein-built meal | Shake? Protein meal? ✓ |
| 5–6 | Shake #2 | All three ✓ |
| 7+ | Snack swaps; start roughly tracking grams | ~160 g average |

Don't count grams for the first few weeks. Just check boxes. Tracking a habit that isn't automatic yet mostly produces guilt.

## Fast Food That Works

Approximate values; check each chain's current nutrition info.

| Order | Protein |
|---|---|
| Chipotle bowl, double chicken | ~60–70 g |
| Chick-fil-A 12-count grilled nuggets | ~38 g |
| Subway footlong, double meat | ~40–50 g |
| Double-patty burger | ~30–40 g |
| Rotisserie chicken (grocery store), half | ~60–70 g |

The fries can stay. Just make sure the order is built around the protein.

---

## Snack Swaps

| Snack | Protein | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Greek yogurt cup | ~15–20 g | Casein-heavy; good before bed |
| Cottage cheese (1 cup) | ~25 g | Same; mix in fruit or honey |
| Beef jerky / meat sticks | ~10–15 g/serving | Shelf-stable; keep in car or bag |
| String cheese | ~7 g each | Easy to stack two or three |
| Protein bars (Quest, Barebells, etc.) | ~20 g | Covers the sweets craving too |
| Hard-boiled eggs | ~6 g each | Batch-cook a dozen on Sunday |
| Protein shake (ready-to-drink) | ~30 g | Fairlife, Premier, etc.; no mixing |

Sweets and chips don't need to go. They just shouldn't be the *only* snacks in the house. At maintenance calories, a daily treat doesn't undo anything as long as the protein anchors are in place.
